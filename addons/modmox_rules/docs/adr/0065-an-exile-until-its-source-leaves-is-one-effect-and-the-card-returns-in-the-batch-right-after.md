# An exile until its source leaves is one effect, and the card returns in the batch right after

Leyline Binding came with Zar Ojanen, Scion of Efrava's deck: "When this enchantment enters, exile target nonland permanent an opponent controls until this enchantment leaves the battlefield." It is the first "until" exile (CR 610.3). Oblivion Ring's is close, but it is two Linked Abilities: an "enters" trigger that exiles and a "leaves the battlefield" trigger that returns the exiled card (CR 607). Built that way, Leyline Binding would play wrongly in two ways. If it left while its trigger was on the stack, the leaving trigger would resolve first and find nothing, and the card would then be exiled for good; by CR 610.3c it must not be exiled at all. And its return would use the stack, so a player could respond between the Binding leaving and the card coming back; by CR 610.3a it comes back immediately, as part of the same effect.

**The exile says it lasts until its source leaves.** `ExileEffect.until_source_leaves` reads "exile [selection] until this permanent leaves the battlefield". When it resolves and its source is no longer on the battlefield, it exiles nothing (CR 610.3c). Otherwise each card it exiles remembers its source in `exiled_with_object_id`, as a linked exile's does, whatever `links_to_source` says, and `returns_when_exiler_leaves` as well, carried on its `ZoneChangeEvent` (`exiled_until_source_leaves`) and recorded as it arrives, as everything an arriving object starts with is (ADR 0008). The flag is copied with the object and is in the fingerprint. A subclass, `ExileUntilSourceLeavesEffect`, was turned down: the exile is the same exile, and the Agent's tag, the selections and the screen already know `ExileEffect`.

**The return comes from the game state, not from an ability.** `ExileUntilRules.returns()` lists every card in exile with the flag whose exiler is not on the battlefield, and `MmGameState` asks it at the end of every batch, after that batch's triggers are found (ADR 0017), and performs what it lists as a batch of its own. So the card comes back immediately after the batch its exiler left in, before state-based actions or priority, whatever made the exiler leave: a destroy, a bounce, or a Wrath that also destroys creatures, which the card then escapes. Because the return is its own batch, the exiler's "leaves the battlefield" triggers look back at a battlefield without the returned card, and the card's own "enters" triggers see it arrive, as two events one after the other would. The batch goes through the same pipeline as any other, so a replacement on the return can still stop for a choice. It returns under its owner's control (CR 610.3c) as a new object, untapped and with summoning sickness. A token exiled this way never returns; it ceases to exist (CR 111.8, 704.5d). Looking at every exiled card after every batch was preferred to listening for the exiler's zone change: it cannot miss a way of leaving, and the exile zone is small.

**Its words.** `AbilityText` adds "until [this permanent] leaves the battlefield" to the exile: "When this enchantment enters, exile target nonland permanent an opponent controls until this enchantment leaves the battlefield."

**The screen needed nothing.** The card remembers what exiled it, so it is tucked behind that permanent as a linked exile's card is (game ADR 0043), and a Tucked Card already means "this comes back when the permanent in front leaves". When the permanent leaves, the card flies from behind it straight to the battlefield, since both happen in one action, where an Oblivion Ring's card first flies to the pile. State Information keeps "Exiled by [name]." by the user's choice.

**The Agent.** It already treats an exile as removal, and aims a triggered ability's target by its effects. That a card can come back is seen in simulation: removing an opposing Binding plays out the return. A permanent spell has no Timing Tag, so with flash it would be considered at every priority. At the user's request, a permanent spell with flash takes the tag of its own "enters" trigger when that is removal or a trick (`AiTiming._tag_of_flash_permanent()`, ADR 0020), so the Agent holds Leyline Binding for its main phase, the other player's end step, blocks and responses, as it holds removal.

Left out until a card needs them (ADR 0001): a returned permanent's "as this enters" choice (ADR 0052), which an Oblivion Ring's return leaves out too; "until" exiles of other durations, such as "until end of turn" or "until your next turn"; exiling "until" from zones other than the battlefield and graveyards; and two "until" exiles on one object telling their cards apart.

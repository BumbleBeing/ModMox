# An attachment points at what it is attached to, and is unattached by a state-based action

Equipment arrived with Short Sword, on the card track. A permanent attached to another (CR 301.5) keeps one number on its Game Object, `attached_to_object_id`: the ID of the permanent it is attached to, or 0 for nothing. The permanent it is attached to keeps no list of what is attached to it. Whoever needs that list works it out from the battlefield, so there is one fact in one place and nothing to keep in step. The number is copied with the object, is in the fingerprint, and is shown in the Object View, where it is public like the permanent.

Attaching and unattaching are events (ADR 0007). `AttachEffect` ("Attach [attachment] to [to]", CR 701.3) proposes an `AttachedEvent` for each selected permanent that may be attached to the one permanent `to` finds and is not attached to it already (CR 701.3a, 701.3b). An attachment that moves needs no second event: being attached to the new permanent is no longer being attached to the old one (CR 701.3c). `UnattachedEvent` leaves the permanent on the battlefield, attached to nothing.

What a permanent may be attached to is asked in one place, `AttachmentRules.can_be_attached()`: both are on the battlefield, they are not the same permanent, an Equipment's is a creature (CR 301.5), and it does not have protection from the attachment (CR 702.16d). The effect asks as it attaches. State-based actions ask again for everything attached, and unattach whatever fails (CR 704.5n). Nothing else unattaches. A creature that leaves the battlefield is retired and a new Game Object takes its card's place (ADR 0008), so the Equipment's number points at an object that no longer exists until the next check, which is before any player has priority; `AttachedToSelection` selects nothing in that moment. An Equipment that leaves the battlefield is a new object too, and a new object is attached to nothing. A list on the creature, cleared as it left, was rejected: the zone change would have to know about attachments, and the state-based action is in the rules anyway.

"Equipped creature" is a Selection (ADR 0014), `AttachedToSelection`: what the ability's source is attached to. "Equipped creature gets +1/+1" is then an ordinary `ContinuousStaticAbility` with an ordinary `ModifyPowerToughnessContinuousEffect`, and anything else an Equipment gives its creature composes the same way with no new ability class.

"Equipped", said of any permanent rather than of what this Equipment is attached to, is a Filter, `IsEquippedFilter`: a permanent on the battlefield with at least one Equipment attached to it (CR 301.5), found by looking for an Equipment on the battlefield that points at it, since the permanent keeps no list. It arrived with Kemba, Kha Enduring, Sunspear Shikari and Resistance Reunited. "Equipped creatures you control get +1/+1" is then a `ContinuousStaticAbility` over an `EachSelection` of equipped creatures you control; "As long as this creature is equipped, ..." is a `SelectionMatchesCondition` on the source; and "Equipped creatures you control gain indestructible until end of turn" a `CreateContinuousEffect` over the same Selection, whose creatures are fixed as it resolves (CR 611.2c). A creature with two Equipment is equipped once. The Filter asks for the Equipment subtype rather than anything attached, so it stays right when Auras arrive. A general "has an attachment matching [filter]" was rejected until a card needs "enchanted" too (ADR 0001).

Equip (CR 702.6) is `EquipAbility`, a subclass of `ActivatedAbility` authored with its mana cost alone. It is made with its target requirement ("creature you control"), its `AttachEffect` from its source to that target, and "activate only as a sorcery" (ADR 0036). Those are ordinary fields of an activated ability, saved with the card, so casting, targeting, protection from being targeted (CR 702.16b), paying and resolving all treat it as any other, and a Modification changes it as any other. The subclass exists so the rules text, the Keyword Chip and the Reminder can name the keyword. A plain activated ability recognized by its shape was rejected: two cards built slightly differently would print differently. Its "only as a sorcery" is lifted for a player who controls an `InstantSpeedEquipAbility` (ADR 0036).

Reconfigure (CR 702.151) arrived with Lion Sash, an Equipment that is also a creature. The keyword stands for two activated abilities (CR 702.151a), and a card with it carries two `ReconfigureAbility`s, each authored with the same mana cost, the second with `unattaches` set. The first is made as equip is, with "another target creature you control" and "only as a sorcery"; the second with an `UnattachEffect` ("Unattach [selection]", CR 701.3d, proposing the same `UnattachedEvent` the state-based action does), no target, "only as a sorcery" and "only while this is attached to a creature" (ADR 0036). Two abilities keep every activation an ordinary `ActivateAbilityAction` on one ability index, so casting, paying, the decision log and the screen treat each half as any ability; one ability with two modes was rejected because the rules give reconfigure no modes, and one ability index offering two actions because it would have reached into the action, the cast procedure and the log. The rules text prints the keyword once, from the first. It is not an `EquipAbility`, so an `InstantSpeedEquipAbility` never lifts its restriction, as the rules say.

`AttachmentRules.can_be_attached()` now refuses an Equipment that is a creature unless it has reconfigure (CR 301.5c). One with reconfigure stops being a creature as it becomes attached, in layer 4 (CR 702.151b, ADR 0018), so the state-based action finds nothing wrong with it, and an Equipment attached to it falls off, since it is no longer a creature. When what it is attached to leaves, the state-based action unattaches it and it is a creature again.

Auras arrived with Nylea's Presence (ADR 0064). An Aura spell enters attached to its target, carried on its zone change, which is the one exception to a new object being attached to nothing. `AttachmentRules.can_be_attached()` also asks the Aura's enchant ability. An Aura attached illegally, or to nothing, goes to its owner's graveyard (CR 704.5m) instead of becoming unattached. `AttachedToSelection` is "enchanted land" on an Aura.

Left out until a card needs it (ADR 0001): "enchanted" as a Filter beside "equipped"; and the new timestamp an Equipment gets as it becomes attached (CR 613.7e), which cannot matter while every Equipment only adds to power and toughness and reconfigure's type change applies before every other one in layer 4.

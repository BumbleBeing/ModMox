# An attachment points at what it is attached to, and is unattached by a state-based action

Equipment arrived with Short Sword, on the card track. A permanent attached to another (CR 301.5) keeps one number on its Game Object, `attached_to_object_id`: the ID of the permanent it is attached to, or 0 for nothing. The permanent it is attached to keeps no list of what is attached to it. Whoever needs that list works it out from the battlefield, so there is one fact in one place and nothing to keep in step. The number is copied with the object, is in the fingerprint, and is shown in the Object View, where it is public like the permanent.

Attaching and unattaching are events (ADR 0007). `AttachEffect` ("Attach [attachment] to [to]", CR 701.3) proposes an `AttachedEvent` for each selected permanent that may be attached to the one permanent `to` finds and is not attached to it already (CR 701.3a, 701.3b). An attachment that moves needs no second event: being attached to the new permanent is no longer being attached to the old one (CR 701.3c). `UnattachedEvent` leaves the permanent on the battlefield, attached to nothing.

What a permanent may be attached to is asked in one place, `AttachmentRules.can_be_attached()`: both are on the battlefield, they are not the same permanent, an Equipment's is a creature (CR 301.5), and it does not have protection from the attachment (CR 702.16d). The effect asks as it attaches. State-based actions ask again for everything attached, and unattach whatever fails (CR 704.5n). Nothing else unattaches. A creature that leaves the battlefield is retired and a new Game Object takes its card's place (ADR 0008), so the Equipment's number points at an object that no longer exists until the next check, which is before any player has priority; `AttachedToSelection` selects nothing in that moment. An Equipment that leaves the battlefield is a new object too, and a new object is attached to nothing. A list on the creature, cleared as it left, was rejected: the zone change would have to know about attachments, and the state-based action is in the rules anyway.

"Equipped creature" is a Selection (ADR 0014), `AttachedToSelection`: what the ability's source is attached to. "Equipped creature gets +1/+1" is then an ordinary `ContinuousStaticAbility` with an ordinary `ModifyPowerToughnessContinuousEffect`, and anything else an Equipment gives its creature composes the same way with no new ability class.

Equip (CR 702.6) is `EquipAbility`, a subclass of `ActivatedAbility` authored with its mana cost alone. It is made with its target requirement ("creature you control"), its `AttachEffect` from its source to that target, and "activate only as a sorcery" (ADR 0036). Those are ordinary fields of an activated ability, saved with the card, so casting, targeting, protection from being targeted (CR 702.16b), paying and resolving all treat it as any other, and a Modification changes it as any other. The subclass exists so the rules text, the Keyword Chip and the Reminder can name the keyword. A plain activated ability recognized by its shape was rejected: two cards built slightly differently would print differently.

Left out until a card needs it (ADR 0001): Auras, whose enchant ability says what they may be attached to and which go to the graveyard when unattached (CR 303.4, 704.5m); Equipment that is also a creature (CR 301.5c); and the new timestamp an Equipment gets as it becomes attached (CR 613.7e), which cannot matter while every Equipment only adds to power and toughness.

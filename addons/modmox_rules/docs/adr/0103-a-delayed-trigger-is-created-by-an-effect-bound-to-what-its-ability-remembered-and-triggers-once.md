# A delayed trigger is created by an effect, bound to what its ability remembered, and triggers once

Harried Dronesmith says "At the beginning of combat on your turn, create a 1/1 colorless Thopter artifact creature token with flying. It gains haste until end of turn. Sacrifice it at the beginning of your next end step." It is the first card with a delayed triggered ability (CR 603.7): "an ability that a spell or ability creates as it resolves, which triggers later on." It needs three things: "it" for the token just created, the delayed ability, and "sacrifice it".

**"It", the token just created, is `MovedCardsSelection`.** `CreateTokenEffect` now remembers the tokens it creates, as a mill remembers the cards it milled (engine ADR 0080's third addendum), so "It gains haste until end of turn" is a `CreateContinuousEffect` of `GainAbilitiesContinuousEffect` on it.

**A delayed trigger is `CreateDelayedTriggerEffect`, an effect holding a `TriggeredAbility`**, as "When you do" is a `ReflexiveTriggerEffect` holding one (engine ADR 0088). Its ability has a trigger of its own, such as a `StepBeganTrigger` for "at the beginning of your end step". As the effect happens it performs a `DelayedTriggerCreatedEvent`, which records a `DelayedTrigger` in `MmGameState.delayed_triggers`. That holds the ability, its controller, and its source's ID, Card Identity, name and ability index, as they were as it was created. It waits in the game state rather than on an object, so it triggers whatever has become of its source since (CR 603.7). A Dronesmith that leaves the battlefield still has its Thopter sacrificed.

**It is bound to what its ability remembered.** The `DelayedTrigger` keeps the `moved_ids` of the context that created it, and the `PendingTrigger` it becomes carries them to its object on the stack (`MmGameObject.moved_object_ids`), from which its context is made. So the delayed ability's "it" is the token created before it, never another permanent (CR 603.7c). A token that has left the battlefield is gone (ADR 0008), so nothing is sacrificed in its place.

**It triggers once, the next time its trigger is met, and is gone.** `TriggerDetection` looks for delayed triggers after the objects' abilities of each batch. One whose trigger matches an event of the batch performs a `DelayedTriggerEndedEvent`, which removes it, and an `AbilityTriggeredEvent` for its ability, which is then put on the stack like any other pending trigger. "Next" is the next to begin: one created during your end step has missed it and waits for your next turn's. Its trigger's "your" is its controller, so an opponent's end step never triggers it.

**"Sacrifice it" is `SacrificeSelectedEffect`** ("sacrifice [selection]", CR 701.21a). The spell or ability's controller sacrifices each selected permanent they still control, all at once, with nothing to choose. `SacrificeEffect` stays the effect where a player chooses by a Filter. Keeping the two apart was chosen over a selection field on `SacrificeEffect`, whose choice, count and players would then mean nothing.

A copy of the game shares its delayed triggers, which never change once made, and the state's fingerprint names each. `AbilityText` says what the delayed ability does and then when: "Sacrifice it at the beginning of your next end step." The remembered token is "it".

**The Agent's attack planner knows a creature is doomed.** Simulation plays the sacrifice, but `AiAttackPlanner` judges each attacker by its own bar and holds creatures back for the swing back. A creature its player's own delayed trigger will sacrifice at the beginning of a step still to come this turn can never block in the other player's turn. So it always attacks, and is never kept home or counted as a defender.

Left out until a card needs them (ADR 0001): a delayed trigger with an intervening "if" or targets of its own; one waiting for something other than a step to begin, such as "when that creature leaves the battlefield"; one that exiles or destroys "it" rather than sacrificing it, which the attack planner would also count as doomed; "them" in `AbilityText` for several tokens created; and showing a waiting delayed trigger on screen.

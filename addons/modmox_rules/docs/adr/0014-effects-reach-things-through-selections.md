# Effects reach players and objects only through Selections

No effect holds a target or a list of objects itself. Every effect names who or what it affects through a Selection. `TargetSelection(n)` means the target chosen for target requirement n, and several effects may share it ("3 damage to target creature and 1 to its controller"). `EachSelection(filter)` means every player or permanent matching a Filter when the effect resolves, without targeting. `YouSelection` means the controller. Target requirements live on the spell or ability, each with a Filter, and Filters compose with AllOf, AnyOf and Not.

This makes "target" versus "each" a data change instead of a code change. An "overload" Modification simply removes the target requirements and replaces each `TargetSelection` with an `EachSelection` using the same Filter, and the spell stops targeting, ignores hexproof, and can no longer fail to resolve for lack of targets. Whether something targets, which mana-ability classification depends on, is worked out from its Selections.

A target requirement also says where its targets are (milestone 13d). By default they are players and permanents; set to spells, it offers the spells on the stack, top first, without the spell it belongs to and never an ability, and its Filter may be left empty to mean any spell. This is a setting on the requirement, not a Filter, because a Filter can only narrow candidates it is handed, and "a creature" must go on meaning a creature on the battlefield (CR 115.4) for every card already saved. A target that has left the stack is illegal when its spell resolves, so `CounterEffect` needs no check of its own beyond finding the spell still there.

Two more places arrived with milestone 13f. Set to cards in graveyards, a requirement offers every card in each player's graveyard, players in ID order and each graveyard in its own order; "target creature card in your graveyard" narrows that with a creature Filter and an `OwnedByFilter`, because a card in a graveyard has an owner and no controller (CR 108.4). Set to abilities, it offers the activated and triggered abilities on the stack, top first, either player's, and never a spell; a mana ability never uses the stack, so it is never one (CR 605.3a). Protection applies to neither, since it works only on the battlefield (CR 113.6). `CounterEffect` removes a countered ability from the stack, and the `AbilityLeftStackEvent` says it was countered; `ReturnToHandEffect` puts a selected card from a graveyard into its owner's hand.

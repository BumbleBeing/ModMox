# Effects reach players and objects only through Selections

No effect holds a target or a list of objects itself. Every effect names who or what it affects through a Selection. `TargetSelection(n)` means the target chosen for target requirement n, and several effects may share it ("3 damage to target creature and 1 to its controller"). `EachSelection(filter)` means every player or permanent matching a Filter when the effect resolves, without targeting. `YouSelection` means the controller. Target requirements live on the spell or ability, each with a Filter, and Filters compose with AllOf, AnyOf and Not.

This makes "target" versus "each" a data change instead of a code change. An "overload" Modification simply removes the target requirements and replaces each `TargetSelection` with an `EachSelection` using the same Filter, and the spell stops targeting, ignores hexproof, and can no longer fail to resolve for lack of targets. Whether something targets, which mana-ability classification depends on, is worked out from its Selections.

A target requirement also says where its targets are (milestone 13d). By default they are players and permanents; set to spells, it offers the spells on the stack, top first, without the spell it belongs to and never an ability, and its Filter may be left empty to mean any spell. This is a setting on the requirement, not a Filter, because a Filter can only narrow candidates it is handed, and "a creature" must go on meaning a creature on the battlefield (CR 115.4) for every card already saved. A target that has left the stack is illegal when its spell resolves, so `CounterEffect` needs no check of its own beyond finding the spell still there.

Two more places arrived with milestone 13f. Set to cards in graveyards, a requirement offers every card in each player's graveyard, players in ID order and each graveyard in its own order; "target creature card in your graveyard" narrows that with a creature Filter and an `OwnedByFilter`, because a card in a graveyard has an owner and no controller (CR 108.4). Set to abilities, it offers the activated and triggered abilities on the stack, top first, either player's, and never a spell; a mana ability never uses the stack, so it is never one (CR 605.3a). Protection applies to neither, since it works only on the battlefield (CR 113.6). `CounterEffect` removes a countered ability from the stack, and the `AbilityLeftStackEvent` says it was countered; `ReturnToHandEffect` puts a selected card from a graveyard into its owner's hand.

A third way to say who or what arrived with Whitemane Lion: `ChosenSelection(filter)`, "a creature you control", one permanent its controller chooses as the effect that uses it begins (CR 608.2d), numbered like a target so several effects may share one. It is not a target (CR 115.10), so protection does not stop it and nothing about it can keep a spell or ability from resolving. The resolution stops for a `ChooseObjectDecision` before the effect's first batch, as it stops for a replacement choice (ADR 0019), and keeps the answer in the effect context, which takes a new copy for each choice so that a copy of the game made mid-choice shares nothing that changes. With nothing to choose from, nothing is asked and the Selection finds nothing. `ReturnToHandEffect` now also returns a selected permanent to its owner's hand, as a new object there (ADR 0008).

"Up to N target" arrived with Felidar Savior and Gird for Battle: "put a +1/+1 counter on each of up to two target creatures" is one requirement, not two, because two requirements would force two targets and allow the same creature twice (CR 115.3). A requirement has `most` and `is_up_to`, and is answered with a `ChooseTargetsDecision` (ADR 0016). Its `TargetSelection` selects each target it chose that is still legal, so the effect applies to each. A stack object's targets stay one flat list, with a place for each target a requirement may choose: every requirement before this took one place, so its place was its index and nothing saved changed. Anything that reads every target of a stack object, such as the `TargetsFilter` of Heroic, sees each one, and an unchosen place stays null. An "up to" requirement can always be chosen, with none, so it never keeps a spell from being cast, a mode from being chosen (CR 700.2a) or a trigger from going on the stack (CR 603.3d). With nothing to choose, a trigger's is not asked, since none is the only answer and a trigger cannot be canceled. A spell's or an activated ability's still is, with no candidates, so that its player confirms choosing none or cancels the cast (CR 728): once a full pick of targets confirmed itself on the Encounter Screen (game ADR 0022), the user wanted a cast with nothing to aim at never to go on the stack without a click. It was first not asked for either. A spell or ability that chose no target at all is not targeting anything, so it resolves (CR 608.2b); one whose chosen targets are all illegal does not. Until a card needs them, "up to" is only for players and permanents, its candidates must be permanents, and more than one target needs "up to": "two target creatures", exactly, waits (ADR 0001). A nested list for each requirement was rejected: Godot has no typed nested arrays, and every reader of the targets would have changed.

# A cost paid on resolution is paid as a cast's mana is, and the X paid is on the effect context

"You may pay {X}. If you do, …" came on the card track with Taj-Nar Swordsmith, a card of the Villain deck `kemba_kha_enduring`: "When this creature enters, you may pay {X}. If you do, search your library for an Equipment card with mana value X or less, put that card onto the battlefield, then shuffle." It is the first mana paid while a spell or ability resolves rather than as it is cast, and the first X that is chosen then (CR 107.3, 608.2d). The card also needs a `ManaValueFilter` whose limit is a Value, which comes next as a primitive of its own; until then the tests use a test-only Filter for "mana value X or less".

**`MayPayXEffect` wraps what "if you do" does, as `MayEffect` wraps what yes does.** Its one field, `effect`, is what happens once X is paid. As it begins, its controller is asked a `ChooseXToPayDecision`: `most` is the largest X `ManaPayment.can_afford()` finds they could pay as generic mana right now, from their pool and the mana abilities they could activate, and the answer is an index (ADR 0012): `DECLINE` (0) or `answer_for(x)` (x + 1) for an X from 0 to `most`. Paying X = 0 is paying, so what "if you do" offers happens: a search for "mana value 0 or less" is still made, and the library is still shuffled. Declining does nothing, and the ability still resolves, so the effects after it happen.

**X is paid one `PayManaDecision` at a time, as a cast's mana is.** The loop that paid a cast's mana moved out of `CastProcedure` into `ManaPaymentProgress` (`src/mana/`): what one player still owes, the `PayManaDecision` for the next step, and how its answer pays, pool mana first and then mana abilities, whose new mana goes straight to the cost (CR 605.3a). `CastProcedure` and `ResolutionProcedure` both pay through it, so paying on resolution is not a second way to pay. `TurnProcedure` hands a `ChooseXToPayDecision`, and a `PayManaDecision` that comes while something resolves, to the resolution. Because `can_afford()` is exact, a payment begun on resolution always finishes; an assert says so, since unlike a cast it could not be rewound (CR 728). Having the engine pay automatically was rejected: the payer chooses which sources to tap, and the Hero's Auto-tap (ADR 0031) and the AI's payment planner already answer `PayManaDecision`.

**Once X is paid, the effect it wraps begins in its place, in a context holding X.** `MmEffectContext` gained `paid_x` (`NONE_PAID`, -1, until something is paid) and `with_paid_x()`, which makes a new context as `with_choice()` does; `with_choice()` keeps it too. `ResolutionProcedure` takes that context for the rest of the resolution and begins the wrapped effect as if it were the current one: its choices, then the decision it asks of its own as it begins, such as a search's, then its batches. So a wrapped search's Filter sees X as it works out what may be found. The choices of the wrapped effect are not asked before X is chosen. `PaidXValue` is "X": the X paid, 0 when none was or with no context at all, and every effect after the payment in the same spell or ability reads it. `AbilityText` words it "you may pay {X}. If you do, [effect]", with no "where X is" after it, since paying {X} says what X is. Nothing on the game state records X: it lives only while the spell or ability resolves, and the procedure's copy carries it (`copy_for()`), so a Simulation Copy made mid-payment carries on alike and a replay of the decision log gives the same game (ADR 0010).

**Only the Villain pays X for now.** Choosing X needs an input on screen first. Until then `DecisionInputs` lists `ChooseXToPayDecision` with no input, and the Encounter Presenter declines it for the Hero without asking, with a warning: a Hero who controls such a card, as through Spoils (ADR 0039), gets nothing from the "if you do", never a question with nothing to click. The engine itself knows nothing of this (ADR 0005), so when the screen gains an input only the presenter changes. Only choosing X will need one: the payment already has its input. The Hero does pay on resolution for ward, through this same loop, with no prototype needed (ADR 0062).

**The AI weighs only the Xs worth paying.** For each X from 0 to `most`, it asks the wrapped effect what it would offer begun with that X paid, on its World; an X is worth paying when it offers something no smaller X does, such as a search finding a card of a higher mana value, and every other X pays more for the same. Its own library is only re-dealt within itself, so it knows what is there, as a player knows their own deck. A CHEAP Agent pays the largest X worth paying, or declines when there is none; a FULL Agent simulates declining and each of them, with the CHEAP answer the default that another must beat, so mana kept for the rest of the turn is weighed against what X buys. For a wrapped effect that asks nothing of its own, only the most is worth paying. `AiTiming` reads through a `MayPayXEffect` as through a `MayEffect`.

Left out until a card needs them (ADR 0001): a cost on resolution other than {X} ("you may pay {1}{W}"), "if you don't", an X with colored mana or a limit, and a "you may" whose effect asks a decision of its own (`MayEffect`'s Later), which can begin it in its place in the same way. "Unless its controller pays [cost]" came with ward (ADR 0062): it asks a yes or no and pays a yes through `ManaPaymentProgress` here.

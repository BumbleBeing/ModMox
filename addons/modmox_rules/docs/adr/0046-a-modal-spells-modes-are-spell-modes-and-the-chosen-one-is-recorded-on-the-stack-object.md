# A modal spell's modes are Spell Modes, and the chosen one is recorded on the stack object

Modal spells arrived with Coordinated Maneuver, on the card track: "Choose one — • Coordinated Maneuver deals damage equal to the number of creatures you control to target creature. • Destroy target enchantment." (read without planeswalkers, game ADR 0040). A modal spell's controller chooses its mode as it is cast, before its targets (CR 601.2b, 700.2).

**A mode is an `AbilityMode`: target requirements and effects of its own.** (It was named `SpellMode` until modal triggered abilities came; see below.) `SpellAbility` gained `modes`; a spell with any is modal, and its own `targets` and `effects` stay empty. `targets_for()` and `effects_for()` give the chosen mode's, or the spell's own for `NO_MODE`, so the procedures and the AI ask one question whether a spell is modal or not. Each mode numbers its targets from 0, so a `TargetSelection` in one mode means that mode's target: exactly one mode is ever chosen. Writing the modes as separate spell abilities on the card was rejected: every reader of "the spell ability" would then have to know which one counts, and a Modification could not tell a second spell ability from a mode.

**The chosen mode is recorded on the spell's Game Object by an event.** Casting has a new first stage: `CastProcedure` asks a `ChooseModeDecision`, whose answer is an index into the modes that may be chosen (ADR 0012), and performs a `ModeChosenEvent`, which sets `chosen_mode` on the stack object as `TargetChosenEvent` sets a target. It is copied with the object, in the decision log by way of the answer (ADR 0010), undone with the cast (ADR 0015), and shown to both players in the Object View, since a mode is announced (CR 700.2). Resolution rechecks and uses only the chosen mode's targets and effects, so a chosen mode whose only target has gone does not resolve (CR 608.2b).

**A mode that could not have every target chosen can't be chosen (CR 700.2a), and one that is the only choice is chosen without asking.** The decision lists only the modes that may be chosen, with what each one's targets could be (`mode_targets`), so a player can see what a mode would reach before choosing it. A modal spell may be cast when at least one of its modes may be chosen. With exactly one, the engine chooses it and asks nothing, at the user's request: there is nothing to decide, and a window offering one choice is a click for nothing. The AI is asked nothing either.

**A modal triggered ability has modes the same way, and its mode is chosen as it is put on the stack.** Modal triggered abilities arrived with Felidar Retreat, on the card track: "Landfall — Whenever a land you control enters, choose one — • Create a 2/2 white Cat Beast creature token. • Put a +1/+1 counter on each creature you control. Those creatures gain vigilance until end of turn." Its controller chooses the mode while putting it on the stack, before its targets (CR 603.3c, 700.2b). `TriggeredAbility` gained `modes`, `targets_for()` and `effects_for()` as `SpellAbility` has them, and `SpellMode` became `AbilityMode`, since a mode is no longer only a spell's; and `MmAbility.modes_or_none()` gives any ability's. The Trigger Procedure puts each trigger on the stack in the order its controller chose (CR 603.3b), then asks that player the same `ChooseModeDecision`, by the same rules: only the modes whose targets could all be chosen, and none asked when only one could be. A modal trigger with no mode that can be chosen is removed instead of being put on the stack, as a trigger with no legal target is (CR 603.3c-d). `ModeChosenEvent` records the mode on the ability's Game Object, and resolution does only that mode. The mode is fixed from then on: the other player sees it on the stack and may respond to it. There is no cast to cancel, so a trigger's mode and targets can't be taken back.

Left out until a card needs it (ADR 0001): choosing more than one mode ("choose two", "choose one or more"), the same mode more than once, modal activated abilities, and modes that add costs (entwine, escalate).

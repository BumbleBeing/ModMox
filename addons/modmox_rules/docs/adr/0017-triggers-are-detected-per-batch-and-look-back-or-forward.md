# Triggers are detected right after each batch of events, looking back or forward

A triggered ability is found the moment its event happens, not later when someone would receive priority (CR 603.2). Right after an event, or a batch of simultaneous events, is applied and logged, the engine checks every working triggered ability against it and records each match as a pending trigger in the game state. The pending triggers go on the stack the next time a player would receive priority, after state-based actions (CR 603.3), in APNAP order with each player ordering their own. Scanning the event log only at that later point was rejected. It would check the game as it is then, so an observer that left the battlefield in between would wrongly miss its trigger, and a permanent that arrived in between would wrongly see an earlier event.

Events that happen at the same moment are performed together as one batch: state-based actions (CR 704.3), all combat damage in a step (CR 510.2), cleanup's damage removal, and an effect's damage to several recipients at once, as in "1 damage to each creature" (CR 120). Draws stay one per batch (CR 121.2). A batch can stop part-way for a replacement choice and carry on later (ADR 0019); triggers are still looked for once, after all of it. Each event in a batch carries the same batch number, and triggers are looked for once, after the whole batch. Leaves-the-battlefield triggers look back in time (CR 603.10a). They check everything that was on the battlefield just before the batch, including what left in it, so a creature that dies alongside others sees them die, and sees its own death. Every other trigger looks at the game after the batch, so permanents entering together see each other (CR 603.6a).

Looking back needs last-known information (CR 608.2h). When an object leaves the battlefield, its characteristics are frozen on it and the engine keeps it, so "that creature's power" and Filters still answer questions about it after it is gone. Every pending trigger records what triggered it ("that player", "that creature") and the Card Identity of its source, so the GUI can point at the card that caused an ability on the stack. An intervening "if" clause is checked both when the ability triggers and when it resolves (CR 603.4).

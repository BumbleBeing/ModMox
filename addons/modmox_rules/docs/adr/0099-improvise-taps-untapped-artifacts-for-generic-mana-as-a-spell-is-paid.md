# Improvise taps untapped artifacts for generic mana as a spell is paid

Sly Requisitioner brought improvise (CR 702.126a): "Your artifacts can help cast this spell. Each artifact you tap after you're done activating mana abilities pays for {1}." It is the first keyword that changes how a cost is paid rather than how much it is.

**Improvise is `ImproviseAbility`, a `StaticAbility` with no fields**, that works only as its card is cast. `CastProcedure.cast_spell()` asks `CastProcedure.improvises()` of the spell's characteristics and sets `ManaPaymentProgress.improvises` on its payment; nothing else turns it on, so an activated ability's mana, a ward cost or a "you may pay {1}" as an ability resolves is never paid this way.

**Tapping an artifact is one step of paying, `ImproviseAction`.** While the payment owes generic mana, each `PayManaDecision` offers, after the pool's units and the mana abilities, one `ImproviseAction` for each untapped artifact the payer controls, in ID order (`ManaPayment.improvisers_of()`). Answering it performs a `TapEvent` of that artifact and takes {1} off what is owed. It never pays a colored or hybrid symbol (CR 702.126b). It is not the artifact's {T}, so an artifact creature with summoning sickness may improvise (CR 302.6), and a "whenever it becomes tapped" ability sees it become tapped. Cost reductions have already come off as the cast began (CR 601.2f), and the spell's mana value is its mana cost's (CR 202.3). A second instance does nothing more (CR 702.126c).

**Whether it can be cast counts the artifacts too.** `ManaPayment.can_afford()` takes `improvises`, and then each untapped artifact the payer controls is a source with one more choice, a unit that pays only generic mana (`MmMana.NO_TYPE`, which `_covers()` matches to no symbol), beside any mana ability of its own: the artifact pays one way or the other, never both. The castable check in `TurnProcedure` and the largest X a spell may be cast with (`CastProcedure.most_x()`) pass it for a spell with improvise.

The rules say the artifacts are tapped "after you're done activating mana abilities": mana abilities are activated before the cost is paid (CR 601.2g-h). The engine activates them during the payment, one step at a time (ADR 0012), and lets the two come in any order. Nothing in the game changes what a mana ability makes when an artifact is tapped, so the order changes nothing; offering improvise only after the last mana ability would trap a player who improvised first with a colored symbol still owed. `Later:` keep them in order once a mana ability counts tapped artifacts.

The Agent needs no rule to choose such a spell, since the engine offers its cast. Its payment planner (`AiPaymentPlanner`) ranks an `ImproviseAction` by what tapping the artifact loses: one with no body to block with and no {T} ability of its own improvises after pool mana and before any land, which keeps its mana for later; one with a {T} ability ranks as another source, and an artifact creature as a creature does. `_unlocks_a_spell()` counts the artifacts for a spell with improvise. Auto-tap pays with the same planner, and the Tap Preview shows an improvising artifact as it shows a tapped source.

In the GUI, an artifact that can improvise glows blue, as a mana source does, and a click taps it (`DecisionClicks`). One that also has a mana ability offers both in the Option Menu; the improvise row reads "Improvise", and the Info Panel "Improvise: Tap this artifact to pay {1}.", as the user chose. `AbilityText` prints the keyword alone, "Improvise". It has no Keyword Chip, the user's choice, as cycling has none: it works only while its card is cast.

Left out until a card needs them (ADR 0001): convoke (creatures, and colored mana), delve (exiling cards from the graveyard), and improvise for anything but a spell.

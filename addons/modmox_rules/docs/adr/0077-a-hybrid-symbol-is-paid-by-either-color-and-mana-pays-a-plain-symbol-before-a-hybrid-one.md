# A hybrid symbol is paid by either color, and mana pays a plain symbol before a hybrid one

Cultbrand Cinder costs {4}{B/R}: the first hybrid symbol (CR 107.4e), which one black mana or one red mana pays. A card with one is both its colors (CR 202.2d) and each hybrid symbol counts one toward its mana value (CR 202.3f).

**A cost keeps its hybrid symbols in a list of their own.** `MmManaCost.hybrid_symbols` holds each as a `Vector2i` of the two `MmMana.Type`s that pay it, (BLACK, RED) for {B/R}, beside `symbols`, which stay plain. Everything that reads `symbols` as one type each, which is most of the engine, the AI and the GUI, stays right without a change, and a cost with no hybrid symbol is saved as before. A pair of numbers rather than a Resource of its own, because `MmManaCost` makes copies of itself (`reduced_by()`), and a script that exports a list of a script class must never name itself (conventions, "The run ends clean"); a pair also shares nothing between two cards' costs. `describe()` writes plain symbols first and hybrid ones after, `{R}{R/W}`.

**A unit of mana pays a plain symbol of its type first, then the first hybrid symbol with its color, then generic mana** (`ManaPayment.symbol_paid_by()`, which returns `PAYS_HYBRID` for the second). Each is never worse than the next: a plain symbol takes one type, a hybrid symbol two, generic any. `PayManaDecision` and `ManaPaymentProgress` carry `remaining_hybrid_symbols`, and a source is offered only while what it makes pays something still owed, so a Forest is not offered for {B/R} once the generic is paid. `ManaPayment.can_afford()` stays exact: it tries both halves of each hybrid symbol. The rules have the caster announce which half they will pay as the spell is cast (CR 601.2b); the engine lets the payment decide instead, which leaves the same choices open and asks nothing more.

**A cost reduction takes a hybrid symbol of its color when no plain one is left.** CR 601.2b's announcement comes before reductions apply (CR 601.2f), so a caster facing a {R} reduction announces red for {B/R}, and the reduction takes it (CR 118.7); `reduced_by()` makes that choice for them. A reduction of a color neither half has comes off generic mana (CR 118.7b).

**The AI counts a hybrid symbol as wanting either color.** The payment planner pays it as a colored symbol, before generic; `colors_needed_by_hand()` and the land search count both its colors (`AiPaymentPlanner.types_wanted_by()`); `_less_one()` takes a unit off as a payment would. Its Timing Tag and simulation need nothing new.

**The card tool reads two-color hybrid symbols** such as {B/R}, and refuses monocolored hybrid ({2/W}) and Phyrexian ones ({B/P}).

**A hybrid card has a Frame of its own** (`FrameSpec.is_hybrid`), as printed hybrid cards in the M15 frame do: a nonland card of exactly two colors with a hybrid symbol of those colors draws the left color's frame, then the right color's through `masks/hybrid_blend`, a band blending across the middle of the card; then the colorless frame's silver title and type bars, and the colorless P/T box. Its crowns blend the same way. A two-color card with no such symbol stays gold, and one whose colors change gets the Frame of its new colors (ADR 0009). The mask is drawn by `tools/art/hybrid_blend_mask.py`. The pip, `pips/br.svg`, is drawn by `tools/art/hybrid_pip.py` from the two plain pips, the circle split on the diagonal with the first color's glyph at the top left; another pair is a run of the script.

Left out until a card needs them (ADR 0001): monocolored hybrid and Phyrexian symbols, adding hybrid mana to a pool (CR 106.8), and two hybrid symbols that share a color ({B/R}{R/G}), where the first one a red unit finds is not always the one it should pay.

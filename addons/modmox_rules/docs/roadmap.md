# Rules Engine Roadmap

The engine is built one milestone at a time. Each milestone is test-first and fully green before the next one begins, and no milestone adds a mechanic before it is needed (ADR 0001). The exact card list and test scenarios for a milestone are agreed at the start of that milestone, not in advance.

1. ✅ **Skeleton**: the addon folder, GUT 9.7.1, a headless test command (`tools/test.sh`), and enforced static typing.
2. ✅ **Core state**: players, zones, Game Objects with zone-change identity (ADR 0008), Encounter Setup, the Game RNG and AI RNG (ADR 0006), opening hands and London mulligans with Free Mulligans.
3. ✅ **Turn structure and priority**: every phase and step, priority passing, an empty stack, and the turn passing. No cards yet.
4. ✅ **Lands and mana**: playing a land, mana abilities, and the mana pool emptying between steps.
5. ✅ **Casting and the stack**: costs, Values, Filters, targets, resolution, and spells fizzling when their targets become illegal. The first real cards are simple burn and draw spells. Mana costs bring color, derived from the cost (CR 202.2). The game will choose each card's frame (color category × creature/noncreature/land) from its characteristics, so the characteristics query must also work on a Run Card outside any encounter, giving deck and reward screens the same answer as the battlefield.
6. ✅ **Creatures and combat**: permanent spells, including flash, power and toughness, summoning sickness, declaring attackers and blockers, combat damage divided freely among blockers, and first strike, double strike and trample. The first creatures are Grizzly Bears, Hill Giant and Elvish Archers.
7. ✅ **State-based actions**: creatures dying from lethal damage or 0 toughness, players losing at 0 life or after drawing from an empty library, the end of an encounter as a win or a draw, and conceding.
8. ✅ **Triggered abilities**: beginning-of-step, zone-change (enters, dies, put into a graveyard) and spell-cast triggers, intervening "if" clauses, batches of simultaneous events with triggers that look back or forward (ADR 0017), last-known information, APNAP stacking with each player ordering their own triggers, Emblems in the command zone, and the cleanup step's extra priority (CR 514.3a). The first artifacts are Ankh of Mishra, Howling Mine and Ivory Tower. Still to come when a card needs them: "you may" triggers and paying costs during resolution, attack, block and damage triggers, delayed, state and reflexive triggers, and triggers from zones other than the battlefield and command zone.
9. ✅ **Static abilities and layers**: the layer engine (ADR 0018) with abilities (layer 6) and power and toughness (7a-7c), static abilities with continuous effects, "until end of turn" effects from spells, characteristic-defining abilities, and the keywords flying, reach, vigilance, haste, deathtouch and defender. Also blocking additional attackers, damage that stays through cleanup, and additional land plays. The first cards are Giant Growth, Crusade (since removed from Content), Serra Angel, Giant Spider, Wall of Stone and Keldon Warlord. Still to come when a card needs them: layers 1-5, dependencies, landwalk, Auras and Equipment, counters, and durations other than "until end of turn". Protection comes with milestone 10.
10. ✅ **Replacement and prevention effects**: replacement and prevention effects applied to events before they happen (ADR 0019), with the affected player choosing their order only when it matters, and batches that stop for that choice and carry on afterwards. Effects propose batches instead of performing them, and damage to several recipients is dealt at once. Prevention for the rest of the turn and "next N damage" shields, regeneration shields, destroy effects and "can't be regenerated", protection (damage, blocking and targeting), indestructible, and mana in activated abilities' costs. The first cards are Fog, Samite Healer, White Knight, Black Knight, Drudge Skeletons and Terror. Still to come when a card needs them: self-replacement effects, "the next time a source of your choice" (Circle of Protection), redirection (Veteran Bodyguard), replacing draws (Island Sanctuary), protection from enchanting, mana abilities with mana in their cost, and "can't be regenerated this turn".
11. ✅ **First playable Alpha subset** against a simple AI Agent (ADR 0020). The engine gains the Agent interface, the Encounter Driver, Simulation Copies (hidden cards re-dealt, the Game RNG reseeded, no undo barriers) and graveyards in the Player View. The AI is its own addon, `addons/modmox_ai/`: a clean-room adaptation of Forge's design with one policy per decision kind, one-step simulation weighed against passing, a Board Evaluator, a Combat Oracle under reimplemented attack and block planners, Timing Tags derived from card effects, a payment planner and moddable AI Profiles. A benchmark measured rollouts at about 16 ms, so Monte Carlo stays in GDScript. The new cards are Shivan Dragon, Frozen Shade, Goblin Balloon Brigade, Uthden Troll, Llanowar Elves, Prodigal Sorcerer, Shatter and nine vanilla creatures, with three 40-card decks in `content/decks/`. Still to decide game-side: what a drawn encounter means for the run (a loss, a win or a replay), and whether a Villain should know the Hero's decklist once runs exist.
12. ✅ **A stronger AI** (ADR 0020): Flat Monte Carlo on priority decisions, on top of the milestone 11 heuristics: the one-step pick, passing and the best other plays rolled out through the next Game Turn on the same re-dealt worlds, keeping the one-step pick unless another clearly beats it. `AiDifficulty` sets the search budget (Easy is milestone 11's AI, Normal 4 × 4, Hard 4 × 12), and an AI Profile can scale it. AI-against-AI Tournaments measure and tune AI settings. Villains never concede. The benchmark measured a Normal decision at a median of 358 ms. The strength gate was not met (Normal scored 52.5% against Easy, not significant), and AI strength is deferred: combat decides most games and is the same at every level, so rolling out attack alternatives is the likeliest next step. During milestone 13 the Agent stopped activating abilities for nothing (ADR 0020): a keyword counts once, an ability is never activated on top of itself, tricks are judged after they wear off, an ability is weighed as a Repeat Plan of up to 8 activations, and evasion is considered before blockers. Easy keeps the old scoring.
13. ✅ **The GUI**: a playable encounter on screen, Hero against an AI Villain, built game-side in sub-milestones. Each one has its own design round, a throwaway prototype the user signs off on, then test-first code. Game-side questions it raises include what a drawn encounter means for the run.
    - 13a. ✅ **Card faces** (`ui/cards/`): Card Faces and Battlefield Faces drawn only from a `CardLook`, made from printed characteristics or a Player View, which gains colors, card types, supertypes, subtypes and mana cost. Frames are worked out as a FrameSpec of layers and baked on the CPU from the committed CardConjurer frames (`assets/card_frames/`), cached by key. Text is pinned to its box (shrinking, or cut short with an ellipsis), in TeX Gyre Schola. Rules text wraps with inline symbols and scrolls once it would shrink below three quarters of its size; reminder text is left off except on basic lands. A Battlefield Face is 110% as wide as tall, with a border cut from its own frame, its name, a 1.6× P/T box tinted for changes, and Keyword Chips that always fit two, each showing its 16×16 Keyword Icon (`ui/sprites/keywords/`, built by `tools/sprites/build.sh`) or letters when it has none; tapped, it turns sideways and darkens under an upright tap symbol. Art is the first printing's, fetched from Scryfall by `tools/art/fetch_art.gd` into `content/art/` with its credits and a focus point set in `tools/card_gallery/`. Crusade was removed from Content. A colored artifact that is not a land is drawn with its colors' frame (gold for two or more), P/T box and crown inside the artifact frame's silver border, which is how an artifact given a color by a Mod looks. Rules text generated from abilities arrived in 13d-1.
    - 13b. ✅ **Graybox layout** (`ui/encounter/`, `ui/menu/`, ADR 0021): every Region of the Encounter Screen, drawn with real Card Faces and Battlefield Faces from a live encounter against an AI Villain. The Encounter Presenter owns the engine, runs the Villain on a worker thread one answer at a time, and hands the screen snapshots; nothing else in `ui/` may mention the engine. `EncounterLayout` works out every Region from the screen's size (1920 × 1080, `canvas_items` stretch, `expand` aspect). The stack is the Stack Window and each player's Emblems open in their own window, both on the shared `ZoneWindow` shell. The Debug Decision Panel answers all twelve decision kinds through five generic forms, and the Glow marks what a decision can choose. Auto-pass (on by default) follows working step stops, the Hero's own spell is passed for them unless Ctrl holds priority, and the mana pool is six buttons that pay with pool mana. A main menu leads to a temporary start form (decks, seed, difficulty, sample Emblems) or straight to an encounter from `-- --hero= --villain= --seed=`. The Hero's piles run library, graveyard, exile down the right column and the Villain's mirror them above, and the Hero's undo, pass and auto-pass controls sit in their own section of the Hero's panel, under the Emblems button. In a nonland row, creatures stand toward the left and middle and other permanents to their right, smaller and set back; each group keeps its faces' size and scrolls sideways, with an arrow at each end it can scroll toward. Once attackers are declared, creatures out of the combat are drawn a fifth smaller and those in it step toward the middle. The engine gained `MmEngine.run_card()` and exiled cards in the Player View, and a permanent's view no longer reads `*/*` once its numbers are worked out. Still to decide game-side: what a drawn encounter means for the run, and whether a Villain should know the Hero's decklist.
    - 13c-1. ✅ **Battlefield: permanents, mana and targets** (ADR 0022): the Hero clicks things. `DecisionClicks` works out what a click on each object or player answers, and the Glow is drawn from it; only what answers takes the mouse, through a `ClickCatcher` that brightens it under the cursor. Three decision kinds have their own input and lost their Debug Decision Panel forms (A243): activating a permanent's ability by clicking it, paying mana by clicking glowing sources and the mana buttons, and choosing a target by clicking a permanent or a player's panel. A permanent offering more than one thing opens the Option Menu where it was clicked. The Info Panel, a new Region by the hand, prompts for every decision and holds Cancel and the reason an answer was refused. Escape opens the Pause Menu (Resume, Concede, Settings grayed out, Quit), during which the presenter collects nothing from the Villain. The hand keeps to the middle between strict bounds, lands of one name stand together, the six piles are evenly spaced, and "The Villain is thinking…" moved into the Villain's panel. Still to come for these inputs: paying mana automatically, real ability text in the Option Menu (done in 13d-1), and a key for Cancel (13g).
    - 13c-2. ✅ **Battlefield: combat** (ADR 0022): the three combat decisions have their own inputs, and their Debug Decision Panel forms are gone, along with its pair-up and split forms. Clicks build a `CombatSelection`, kept outside the redrawn nodes, which the pass button confirms ("Attack with 2", "Confirm blocks", "Assign damage") and undo takes back a click at a time. Attackers are chosen by clicking creatures, which step into the lane upright; "Declare All" is in the Info Panel. A blocker is picked up with one click and paired with the attacker clicked next, and one click on a creature that already blocks takes it off; Block Lines join blockers to attackers, ending on a scroll arrow when a creature is out of sight. Damage is divided with a Damage Stepper on each recipient, from a default split that kills as many as it can, with the attacked player's portrait as a recipient under trample. Creatures the engine has in combat stand to the left of their row in object ID order. The Villain's combat is drawn the same way, and the "ATTACKING" and "BLOCKING" tags are gone. A player who can be chosen glows and is clicked on their portrait only. Still to come: showing marked damage and animating creatures as they change places (13d).
    - 13d-1. ✅ **The Stack Window** (ADR 0022, ADR 0023): the stack is read and answered in its window. Counterspell joined the game first, as engine work: a target requirement can ask for spells on the stack (`TargetSpec.among`), `CounterEffect` puts a spell into its owner's graveyard with the zone-change reason `COUNTER`, the Agent has a counter Timing Tag and a Cheap Agent counters by cost, and two copies replaced two Gray Ogres in `blue_red`. The Player View shows a stack ability's ability and what triggered it, and an order-triggers decision says the same of each trigger. Rules text is now generated from abilities by `AbilityText`, brought forward from 13h, and printed on Card Faces and in the Option Menu. Each object on the stack has a Stack Color; an entry shows what an ability does or a spell's name, then its controller and targets, each line cut with an ellipsis; the entry under the cursor draws Stack Marks (source solid, targets dashed) and is shown in full in the Info Panel. A spell that can be targeted is clicked on its entry, and a curved arrow runs from a spell to the spell it targets. Triggers are put in order above the stack with a Trigger Order, and a choice between replacement effects is made in a window of its own, so the Debug Decision Panel lost its put-in-order form and both decisions. Still to come: dragging to reorder triggers (13e-2), and the graveyard glow and countering abilities, each with its first card (13f).
    - 13d-2. ✅ **Pacing and Face Motion** (ADR 0027): the game can be followed. The presenter holds back the Villain's next answer until the last snapshot has been up long enough, by what it showed: 500 ms after an action, 270 ms after only tapping for mana, nothing after bookkeeping, with thinking counted toward the wait and the Hero never kept waiting. The screen still rebuilds its board from every snapshot; a `FaceMotion` kept outside the redrawn nodes remembers where each permanent's face was, and each eases to its new place, size and turn over a quarter of a second. Arrivals fade in, and a permanent that leaves fades out as a ghost. An undo and a resized window move nothing. A creature shows the damage marked on it on a Damage Chip over its toughness. Both are off unless the screen turns them on, so tests and screenshots never wait. Cards fly between zones (Card Flights): a card that changes zones is drawn once, in the air, from where it was to where it lands, over every window, in 0.35 seconds, changing from the face it left as into the face it lands as; one put on the stack flies to the art of its Stack Window entry and fades out there, a permanent that flies away leaves no ghost, and nothing flies for a rewind or through the pregame. A Game Speed (Slow, Normal, Fast, Blazing; ADR 0027 has what each is) scales every wait, move, flight and fade together; it is chosen on the Pause Menu's new settings page, applies at once, and is kept in `user://settings.cfg`. Still to come: dealing and mulligans through the air.
    - 13e-1. ✅ **Playing from the hand** (ADR 0022): a card is cast, or played as a land, by dragging it out of the hand or double-clicking it, and one click does nothing. A press becomes a drag after three pixels. The held card (the Drag Card) follows the cursor upright above everything; over the hand's strip the other cards make room for it and letting go puts it down there, and above the strip a card that can be played glows brighter, the Info Panel says "Release to cast …", and letting go plays it. A card that cannot be played right now goes back without a word, as does one moved over the left panels, right-clicked, or held when the Pause Menu opens. A card with several ways to be played waits where it was let go while the Option Menu asks which. The hand is kept in the Hero's own order (the Hand Order), by Card Identity, so a canceled cast or an undo puts a card back where it was. Nothing is rebuilt between picking a card up and letting it go. Casting and playing a land left the Debug Decision Panel, which no longer shows at priority.
    - 13e-2. ✅ **The opening hand and choosing cards** (ADR 0030, ADR 0022): every decision has an input of its own, and the Debug Decision Panel is deleted. Through the pregame the opening-hand overlay covers everything right of the sidebar and holds the Hero's hand, drawn large and fanned as it is in play, in one row up to eight cards and two from nine, the card under the cursor coming to the front and standing upright: playing first or drawing, keeping or mulliganing ("Keep 6", "Free Mulligan") and putting cards on the bottom are answered there, it says who plays first and what the Villain has decided, the Emblems windows open over it, and it fades out when the first turn begins. A card put on the bottom leaves the row and undo brings it back; keeping is final. The engine gained what the overlay needed: a decision says whether its chooser was picked at random and whether the next mulligan is free, each choice to keep or mulligan is an event as it is made (`KeepOrMulliganEvent`), and a hand that has paid a mulligan for every card it holds is kept without asking, its owner still choosing the order it goes to the bottom in. Cards to discard are chosen in the hand's strip with single clicks, a chosen card standing above the row with a white Glow, and the pass button confirms; both choices build a `CardPicks`. Triggers are dragged into order in a `TriggerList` in place of the ▲ and ▼ buttons, one drag being one step for undo. `DecisionInputs` lists what answers each kind of decision, and a test fails when the engine gains a kind with no input. Still to come: cards flying between zones, and a list of triggers that scrolls by itself.
    - 13e-3. ✅ **Paying mana automatically** (ADR 0031): with Auto-tap on, which it is unless turned off with the Auto-tapping box on the Pause Menu's settings page, the Encounter Presenter pays the Hero's mana costs, for spells and abilities alike, and the Hero is not asked. It chooses each source as the Villain's Agent does (`AiPaymentPlanner`), which now taps a land before a creature for a colored symbol too. Mana already in the pool is spent first, so lands tapped by hand before casting are the ones used; a cast that reaches payment with the Hold Key down is paid by hand, all of it, as is a payment that undo leads back to. The taps are shown one at a time, the mana wait apart at the Game Speed in use: the Info Panel reads "Paying for …" with what is owed, Cancel takes the whole cast back, and the sources still to come keep glowing. Before that, the Tap Preview: with the cursor on a card of the hand, a held card, a permanent's ability or a row of the Option Menu that costs mana, only what would be tapped keeps its blue Glow and the pool mana that would be spent glows on its button. A plan is what the payment then does, worked out on a copy of the game when the cursor asks for it.
    - 13e-4. ✅ **Yield Turn** (ADR 0021): the auto-pass box on the Hero's panel is now Yield Turn, which passes every priority for the rest of the turn, stops and the Villain's spells included, and ends by itself when the next turn begins; attackers, blockers and every other choice that is not priority are still asked. Auto-pass itself is a box on the Pause Menu's settings page, kept in the settings file with the Game Speed. The Hold Key (Ctrl) now stops every pass made for the Hero while it is down, not only the one after their own spell, and a priority kept that way is theirs until they answer.
    - 13e-5. ✅ **The opening hand moves** (ADR 0030): on the opening-hand overlay a new hand is dealt from the library a card at a time, turning face up; a mulliganed hand flies back before the next is dealt; a card put on the bottom slides to the library while the row eases closed, and undo brings it back; the card under the cursor rises and grows a little as well as standing upright; and when the first turn begins the hand flies down to its strip while the scrim fades. The moves are kept in `OpeningMotion`, outside the cards the overlay rebuilds, and scale with the Game Speed. The overlay's five buttons are painted by `ButtonShading`: rounded, rimmed, shadowed and lit from above, Keep blue, Mulligan orange, play or draw blue-gray, Confirm green and gray until enough cards are down. A later polish pass (A561 to A563): the scrim is 80% opaque, the heading is bold on a dark gray rounded plate that hugs it, and while play or draw is chosen the plate and the buttons are one group about the middle of the window.
    - 13f. ✅ **Zone viewers** (ADR 0032, ADR 0014): every pile opens a Zone Window when clicked, an empty one included: both graveyards and both exiles as a grid of Card Faces that wraps to the window's width, the newest card first, and both libraries as one row scrolled sideways, top card on the left, with a card back for each card the Hero does not know. The engine now remembers which players know a card in a library (one put there from a hand, until a shuffle), so a card the Hero put on the bottom in the pregame is face up at the right of the row, and the Player View lists each library. Each window is titled with its player's name, the zone and its count, opens beside its pile and reopens where it was left; the Emblems windows show each Emblem's generated rules text on one line, cut with an ellipsis. Players have names, from the start form or `--hero-name=` and `--villain-name=`, used on the panels, stack entries, prompts and windows; without one a player goes by "Hero" or "Villain". A target requirement can ask for cards in graveyards or for abilities on the stack. Raise Dead is the first card to target a card in a graveyard: the pile glows, its window opens by itself when nothing on the board answers (and on a click of the pile when something does), the cards that answer glow and are clicked there, and the window closes once the target is chosen. Stifle (the first card outside Alpha, in `content/cards/scourge/`) is the first to counter an ability, chosen on its Stack Window entry like a spell. Two of each replaced a Pearled Unicorn and a Scathe Zombies in `white_black` and the Gray Ogre and a Wall of Stone in `blue_red`. Left for later: activating an ability from inside a window, which arrives with milestone 14 for an Emblem and with its first card for one that works from a graveyard, and the Agent using what it knows of its own library.
    - 13g. ✅ **The sidebar** (ADR 0034): each panel shows its player's Portrait, a 32 × 32 pixel picture drawn six times over with hard edges: the white wizard for the Hero and the purple demon for the Villain until each has their own (`PlayerPortraits`, A474), with a smaller, more see-through life total in its corner. The step buttons keep their names and get no icons (A476: the user chose text over the 16×16 icons this entry first asked for), in a column narrowed from 112 to 98 pixels, which is as narrow as still fits "Begin Combat" (A483). A pale Glow round the whole panel marks the player with priority, in place of the outline, and the step it is now glows on the active player's row, over the green of a stop if it has one (A477, A478); both ease at the Game Speed. The Pass Button left the Hero's panel for a place of its own over the Info Panel, as wide, gray and shaded, always there: "Pass Priority [SPACE]", or the confirm text of a choice being made, which ends with the key as well ("Attack with 2 [SPACE]", A489, A490, A495); the Info Panel is shorter for it and puts a refused answer's reason beside its Cancel button. The Hero's panel gained a Potions button under the Emblems button, in its style, purple with Potions and gray with none, which counts them and takes no press yet (A491), and under it Undo over Yield Turn fill the rest of the panel. Yield Turn clicked while attackers or blockers are being declared now confirms them as they stand and yields (A494), and the Info Panel's "Declare All" reads "Undeclare All", putting every attacker back, while any is declared (A496). Four Key Bindings can be changed, on the Controls Page that a Controls button on the Pause Menu's settings page opens: pass (Space), undo (Z), cancel (X, new, doing what the Info Panel's Cancel button does when it shows) and holding priority (Ctrl). A clicked row takes the next key pressed, any single key but Escape; two actions may share a key, their rows turn red, and a press does the first of pass, undo and cancel (A480, A486). The Pass, Undo and Cancel buttons name the key bound, and the bindings are kept in `user://settings.cfg`. Left for later: choosing a Portrait, more Portraits, and Potions themselves (milestone 14's design round).
    - 13h. ✅ **The Inspector** (ADR 0033): a right-click pins a card, and the Inspector takes the sidebar's place and shows the card in full: its Card Face with a glow in the color of its Rarity, a View Original button that shows its Card Definition instead, a State List (summoning sickness, damage marked, and each effect or shield on a permanent with the card it came from) and a Reminder for each keyword, the two lists in scrolling sections of their own. The Pinned card is ringed where it is drawn; a second right-click, one on empty space, Escape or a left-click anywhere else lets it go, and that left-click still does what it does. Hovering shows nothing: an Inspector that followed the cursor was built first and was too busy over the hand (A472). Nothing is pinned while a card or a trigger is dragged. It works on the battlefield, the hand, the stack, Emblems, pile windows and the opening hand, and eases in, out and between cards with the Game Speed. Rarity joined the game as a field on the Card Definition, each card given the rarity of its first printing. The card under a right-click is found from where things are drawn, so no face takes the mouse for it. Still to come: Reminders for terms that are not keywords, counters and attachments in the State List when the game has them, and Mythic cards.
    - 13i. ✅ **Stage backdrops** (ADR 0035): the Encounter Screen draws a Backdrop behind everything, one whole 320 × 180 picture drawn six times over with hard edges and never tiled (A504, A511). There are six: `plain`, the slate the screen always had and the default, and one for each color. Stages are not built, so the Encounter Request names the Backdrop, from `--backdrop=` or a choice on the start form, and an unknown name is refused (A498). A Backdrop is the picture `<name>.png` in `ui/sprites/backgrounds/Encounters/` if there is one, and otherwise a placeholder drawn by a shader on the same grid: a horizon over fields for white, sea and cloud for blue, a marsh with bare trunks for black, peaks for red and trunks under a canopy for green (A497, A508). It is built once, outside the redrawn board, under a dim layer of the slate at 55% that keeps faces, Glows and labels readable over any picture; the plain one has no dim layer and looks as before (A506). The game is now always drawn at 16:9: the stretch aspect is `keep`, with black bars on a window of another shape, and the window cannot be made smaller than 1280 × 720 (A505, A513). The layout's handling of other shapes was left in place, unreached (A514). A Zone Plate, a rounded and mostly see-through gray rectangle, lies behind each row of the battlefield and each pile, over the Backdrop, and the hands have none. Left for later: the user's own art in place of the placeholders, a Backdrop for each Tier, motion, and a Stage setting the Backdrop once runs exist.
14. ✅ **Activatable Emblems and Potions** (engine ADR 0036, game ADRs 0032 and 0033; A516 to A538): activating an ability from the command zone, two Activation Restrictions, and Potions. An Emblem is never used up: its abilities are static, triggered or activated, and the Emblem stays for the next encounter. What is gone for good once used is a Potion, an Item of the game's (A491 to A493). Built in the agreed order (A529): the engine test-first, then a throwaway GUI prototype the user signed off on with a green Glow, hover brightening and a beige backing for the Potion Icon (A537, A538; kept in `prototypes/emblems_and_potions/`), then the GUI test-first. Left for later: Items held between encounters, so a used Potion is not yet deleted from anything; Emblem art and the Emblem Face's border, which the user will supply; and a rule for the Agent to save a once-a-game ability or a Potion for a better moment. A code review before closing found three faults in edge cases, all fixed with tests: a dismissed Option Menu could claim the next menu's choice for an Emblem, a Potion's ability pinned from the Stack Window read with no text, and a window could reopen on an unrelated undo.
    - **The Boons** (A517), as files in `content/emblems/`. Diviner's Boon: "{2}{U}: Draw two cards. Activate only as a sorcery and only once each game." Electromancer's Boon: "{R}: This emblem deals 3 damage to any target. Activate only once each game." Others may later cost {0}.
    - **The Potion** (A516, A524): a mock, Big Gulp, "Target creature gets +2/+2 until end of turn." No mana cost, Common, usable whenever the Hero has priority. `--potions` and a start-form checkbox give the Hero one each encounter; nothing holds Items between encounters yet, so nothing is deleted when it is used.
    - **Engine**: an Emblem's ability is offered, paid for and put on the stack like a permanent's, so Stifle counters it (A518). "Only once each game" and "only as a sorcery" are fields on the ability; the source's Game Object marks an ability Spent when it is activated, and the Object View shows it (A518, A519). A Potion is a command-zone object from a list of its own in the setup, with one activated ability whose cost uses it up; it is gone even if the ability is countered, the Player View lists Potions apart from Emblems, both public, and the engine reports which were used (A521 to A523). The Agent weighs these as it weighs a permanent's ability, with no rule for saving them (A527). `--emblems` gives the Hero Howling Winds and both Boons, and the Villain Ankh's Curse and Electromancer's Boon.
    - **Engine tests**: Electromancer's Boon offered only when {R} can be paid; it uses the stack and deals 3 to a creature or a player; never offered again once activated, even when Stifled; Diviner's Boon offered only at sorcery timing and draws two; two Emblems with the same ability each keep their own use; an unrestricted Emblem ability repeats; an Emblem cannot be targeted, stays, and its view marks the Spent ability; Big Gulp is offered at any priority with a creature to target, costs no mana, leaves at once and gives +2/+2 until end of turn; not offered with no creature; gone even when Stifled; the Player View lists Potions apart from Emblems and the engine reports the used ones; undo restores a use or a Potion and a replay reaches the same state; the Agent activates Electromancer's Boon at a good target, and a soak run with this content finishes clean.
    - **GUI** (ADR 0032): an entry in the Emblems or Potions window that can be used glows and takes a click, and several usable abilities open the Option Menu. The window closes at that click, target or none, and reopens where it was if the activation is canceled or undone (A528). A Spent ability is gray and marked "(used)" (A520). The Emblems button glows while something could be activated, is gold while any Emblem is not used up, and gray otherwise; the Potions button glows, is purple with any and gray with none, and opens the Potions window (A526). An Emblem's ability or a Potion is a play for auto-pass, as an instant in hand is, which reverses what this entry used to say (A525). Mana costs are paid with Auto-tap and shown by the Tap Preview.
    - **Pictures** (ADR 0033): a Potion has a 16 × 16 Potion Icon from `tools/sprites/build.sh`, shown on its entry, on its ability's entry in the Stack Window and enlarged in the Inspector with its Rarity glow, name, the type line "Potion" and rules text (A530 to A532). An Emblem is shown in the Inspector on an Emblem Face: art in a border of its own, name, the type line "Emblem", rules text and the artist's credit, with no Rarity glow; its entries show the art cropped (A533, A535). Emblem art lives in `content/art/emblems/` with its own manifest; until the user supplies it the art is a gray box and the border a plain gray frame (A534). The type lines are the GUI's: neither is a card type (A536).
    - **GUI tests**: `DecisionClicks` answers a click on an Emblem or Potion entry and opens the Option Menu for several abilities; usable entries glow, Spent text is gray, the Villain's entries take no click; both buttons show their three states and the Potions count; the Potions window shows each Potion's icon (or the bottle), rules text and Rarity; a window closes at the activating click and reopens on cancel or undo; auto-pass stops at a stopped step when an Emblem ability or a Potion is the only play; an Emblem's cost uses Auto-tap and the Tap Preview; the generated rules text reads exactly as the two Boons are written above; the Stack Window entry of a Potion's ability shows its icon; the Inspector shows a Potion and an Emblem Face as described, and an Emblem without art shows the gray box and no credit.
    - **Prototype**, with made-up data: both buttons in all three states, the Emblems window with glowing, plain and "(used)" entries, the Potions window with Big Gulp, a window closing as an ability is started, the Potion in the Inspector and the graybox Emblem Face.
15. ✅ **The Deckbuilder** (game-side, ADRs 0028, 0029 and 0037; A539 to A565): the screen where the Hero builds Decks from their Collection, applies Mods, adds basic lands, and saves to the Deckbox. It follows milestone 13's process: a design round (done), a prototype the user signs off on (all eight steps built and signed off, A565), then test-first code. The milestone closed on that sign-off: the port into production code is deferred to the first run milestone, since there is no real Collection, Deckbox, Run Card identity or save file to build it on yet (A539). Milestone 13 closed with 13i; what its entries leave for later is refined as content is added (A503). The prototype lives in `prototypes/deckbuilder/`, reuses `ui/cards/`, runs on a made-up Collection (three copies of every Content card, Mods that each add a mana symbol, a few cards already Hexed or full) and saves nothing to disk, but is built to be ported: all its logic is in a Working Deck class with no nodes, and the screen only draws it and reports input. It opens from a main menu button or `-- --deckbuilder`, which honors `--screenshot=`. The screen at 1920 × 1080: an inspector down the left, the Collection as a scrolling grid across the top right, eight deck columns across the bottom right (mana value 0 to 5, 6+, lands), and a top bar with the name box, card count, five basic land buttons that each show how many cards of exactly that name are in the Deck, a zoom slider for the Collection and one for the Deck, Sort, Save and Exit. Zooming the Deck widens its columns, and the strip they stand in scrolls sideways when they no longer fit. A column fans its cards so each shows its name strip, scrolls when overfull, and raises the card under the cursor. The columns are free piles: a card stays in the column and at the place in it where it was dropped, and nothing sorts them while building. A Deck opens sorted (each card in its own mana value column, then WUBRG, multicolor, colorless, then by name), and the Sort button does the same on request. A held card opens a gap in the column under it, at the place it would drop to. Left-click drags (Collection to column, column to column, out of the columns to remove) and double-click adds a card to the bottom of its own column or removes it; right-click selects a card, holding it in the inspector, and a second right-click, one on empty space or Escape clears it. While a card is selected the inspector shows only that card; with none selected it shows the card under the cursor (A563). Cards in the Deck stay in the Collection grid, dimmed. The filter bar carries over MTG Arena's basic filters under a new look approved as a picture before it is wired: search (name, type line, Rules Text), the five colors, multicolor, colorless, card type and mana value, colors combining as "any of these" (but with multicolor on, a card must be multicolor and have every chosen color), and a placeholder for rarity. Every face, a basic land's too, shows its three Mod Slots as bubbles, filled green for a Mod and red for a Hex. The inspector's Apply a Mod button opens a modal with a tile per kind of Mod held, inapplicable ones gray; hovering a tile shows the card before and after, and Apply asks "are you sure". Save keeps the screen open and refuses only an empty or taken name; Exit asks only when there are unsaved changes. Built in seven steps, each shown before the next: the Working Deck and its tests; the layout with real faces, nothing clickable; the filter bar's look, then its wiring; dragging, double-clicking and land buttons; selection, the inspector and Mod Slots; the Mod pop-up; Save and Exit. An eighth step followed the user's review (A547). Out of scope: the Deckbox screen (choosing, creating and deleting Decks, setting the Active Deck), the Hall of Fame, and real Mods.
    - **Step eight** (A547), built after the user's review of the first seven:
        - **The inspector** (A552 to A555, A562): under the face, side by side, a View Original button and a Reminders button. View Original is gray for a card with no Mods or Hexes, toggles as the encounter's does and is let up for another card. Under them two bordered panels that are always drawn and equally tall, each half the height between those buttons and Apply a Mod, which stands by itself at the bottom of the inspector. Card Text has the card's rules text with no reminder text, mana symbols drawn, and a dim "None" for a card with none. Mods has the three bubbles, shaded as on a card's face, in a shaded gray tray with rounded ends twice a bubble's height, and under it a subpanel for each Mod or Hex in slot order (its name over its text, tinted green or red), with nothing there when the card has none. While Reminders is pressed one Reminders panel stands in place of both, with an entry for each keyword from the same `Reminders` the encounter uses; it stays pressed from card to card. What does not fit in a panel scrolls inside it. Apply a Mod is gray when the card is not selected, when all three Mod Slots are filled, or when no held Mod fits the card. With no card the inspector says, centered, "Hover over any card to view it here." and under it "Right-click any card to select it."
        - **The Mod pop-up** (A564): a window no larger than what is in it, with "Modify" and the card's name in bold, centered, at the top. Under it three bordered panels of one height, a card's and a little more, each with its title centered: Valid Mods (every kind of Mod held, with its count, on a dark shaded tile; one that does not fit the card is gray), Current (the card as it is) and "With selected Mod", which reads "With" and the Mod's name once one is hovered over or clicked and is empty until then. The faces there show no Mod Slots. Apply is always in the footer, gray until a Mod is clicked. The footer starts with "Click a Mod to select it. Applying a Mod cannot be undone."
        - **Modifiable basic lands** (A558, ADR 0028): a basic land has Mod Slots and bubbles like any card and is modified by selecting it in the Deck. Its first Mod makes that copy a Run Card the Collection owns: it shows in the Collection, is saved in a Deck as a card, and comes back to the Collection when taken out. One rule decides which Mods fit every card. None of the sample's Mods fits a land, so in the prototype this is covered by tests with a test-only Mod (A560).
        - **Modded Names** (A559, ADR 0029): each Mod's and Hex's name goes in front of the card's, in slot order, on the face, in the inspector, and in search and sorting. The sample's rename Mods are gone, since a Mod's name is now the word it adds. The user's fuller naming rules are still to be agreed.
        - **The look** (A557, A561): asked for as a workbench in shades of wood, and settled, after a round of pictures, as dark maroon. The bench behind everything is darkest, panes are mid, and deck columns and the Mod pop-up's panels a step lighter. Every pane, deck column, inspector panel and the Mod pop-up has a thin gray border, two pixels wide. Every button is gray and shaded, with a dark lip underneath, the filter bar's toggles and chips included; a chosen chip is brass. Text is gray, never a shade of the background. Text boxes are dark gray. Each basic land button keeps its land's color, with its name and count on a white plate at a quarter strength, lettered in near-black. Zoom and its two sliders stand on a gray plate as tall as those buttons. Card faces, the green and red of Mods and Hexes and the mana colors keep their colors. All of it is in `DeckbuilderLook`.
        - **Easing** (A565): the inspector and the windows ease with the encounter's own `Easing`, half again as slowly (`Pacing.EASE_SECONDS` × 1.5). A change of card eases the old one out and the new one in; the Mod pop-up and the unsaved-changes prompt fade in and out, and a closing one is gone at once to the mouse. Tests and pictures give no time, so there everything comes and goes at once.
        - **In the encounter** (A553, ADR 0033): the Inspector's button now reads View Original and its keyword section is titled Reminders, as in the deckbuilder. Giving the encounter's Inspector the deckbuilder's layout (bordered, titled panels, with its State List where the Mods panel is) is a later GUI step of its own (A556).
        - `-- --deckbuilder --select="Card Name"` has that Deck card selected, and `--mods` with it opens the Mod pop-up, for pictures.
    - **Decided for the game around it**:
        - A Deck has no maximum size and there is no copy limit (A540, A541). An illegal Deck can be saved but not played.
        - The deckbuilder is reached only through the Deckbox, which opens from Stage Navigation and by itself before an encounter once the Villain is revealed. There it has a Start button, disabled while the Active Deck is illegal, which is the one place legality is enforced; from Stage Navigation it has a plain Close (A543, ADR 0037).
        - A Run Card that is sold or lost drops out of every saved Deck. The warning before the sale names the Decks it is in and marks the Active Deck with "(Active)" (A542).
        - Mods are applied only in the deckbuilder; the Workshop and Spoils hand them out (A544). There is no undo within the Working Deck (A545). The inspector shows cards and Mods only, not Potions or other Items (A546).
    - **Left for later, with its owner** (A548 to A551):
        - The first run milestone: the port of the deckbuilder into production code, the Deckbox screen, where a Run Card's identity comes from, how the Collection and Deckbox are saved (as run state, not as logged decisions), building the encounter's `MmPlayerSetup` from the Active Deck, the rarity filter (wired with the rarities that exist then; no Mythic is made for it), and the screen's keys looked up in Key Bindings (ADR 0034).
        - The milestone that builds Spoils and the Workshop: real Mods, and the naming rules for Modded Names.
        - A later milestone about what lasts between runs: the Hall of Fame.

The GUI is game-side work, outside the engine: it drives encounters through the Encounter Driver and shows each player their Player View.

# The pregame is answered on an overlay that holds the hand

Until 13e-2 the pregame was answered in the Debug Decision Panel, from lists. Now the opening-hand overlay covers everything right of the sidebar from the first pregame decision until the first turn begins. The Hero's hand is drawn on it large, in the Hand Order, with a heading above and buttons below, and the hand's own strip is empty while it is up (A384). Playing first or drawing, keeping or mulliganing and putting cards on the bottom are all answered there. What it says is worked out by `OpeningHand`, a pure class that reads each snapshot once and remembers what the pregame has said so far; `OpeningHandOverlay` is the node that draws it and reports what was pressed, and the Encounter Screen answers. With these four kinds done every decision has an input of its own, and the panel and its forms are deleted (ADR 0021).

**It covers all but the sidebar, and stays up.** A Status is an Emblem, and one that makes the hand smaller or grants a Free Mulligan bears on whether to keep, so the sidebar is left live: the Emblems buttons work and their windows open over the scrim, and the Inspector takes the sidebar's place while a card of the opening hand is pinned, as it does in play (A392, A403, A469, ADR 0033). Everything under the scrim is dead to the mouse. The overlay stays through the Villain's decisions too, with no buttons, because the Hero may keep, wait for the Villain and only then be asked to put cards down, and closing and reopening it between those would flicker (A388). When the first turn begins its scrim, words and buttons fade out over a quarter of a second while the hand flies down to its strip, with no extra hold, since the Hero is never made to wait (A410).

**It says what the snapshot alone does not.** The Player View does not say who plays first, and a decision does not say how the Hero came to choose. So the overlay reads the starting player from its event and keeps saying it under the heading (A386), and `ChoosePlayOrDrawDecision` says whether its chooser was picked at random, for "You won the coin flip" against "You choose" (A385). `KeepOrMulliganDecision` says whether the next mulligan is free, so the button can read "Free Mulligan" (A387, A399). The heading counts only the mulligans that cost a card, which is also how many cards the hand owes, and the keep button reads the size the hand will be once it has put those down: "Keep 6" over seven cards (A396, A405).

**What the Villain decided is an event.** Keeping had no event at all, and a mulligan's event comes only when the whole round resolves, which can be after the Hero has put cards down. The prototype read the engine's private state to say "Villain has kept a 7 card hand". The engine now performs a `KeepOrMulliganEvent` the moment either player chooses, with the choice and how many cards a kept hand owes, so the screen goes on reading only snapshots (A411, ADR 0021). The existing `MulliganEvent` stays where it is, since it is what counts the mulligan.

**A hand that owes every card is kept without asking.** A player who has paid a mulligan for each card in their hand could mulligan again and be no worse off, forever. The engine does not ask them: it keeps the hand and goes straight to putting it on the bottom, where the player still chooses the order (A412). It is a rule in the pregame for both players, not something the presenter answers for the Hero, so the Villain's Agent is bound by it and a replay's decision log holds no keep that nobody chose. Free Mulligans do not count toward it.

**Keeping is final, and the cards put down simply leave.** Once the Hero keeps there is no way back to the mulligan choice: the engine would allow the undo when the Villain has already decided, but the Villain may have decided on the strength of it, so in the bottoming frame undo takes back only cards put down (A406). Bottoming is a frame of its own after the keep, as the engine asks it (A397). A clicked card leaves the row for the library, the row eases closed, and undo brings the last one back to its place from wherever it has got to. The cards go down in the order clicked, the last one lowest, and a line under the heading says so when more than one is owed: "The last card clicked -> bottom of library". The prototype tried a pile of the cards put down, in three forms with arrows and labels, and the user found each one worse than a line of text; nothing in the game looks at the bottom of a library yet, so there is nothing more to show (A391). The picks are a `CardPicks`, the same class that holds cards chosen to discard (ADR 0022).

**Up to eight cards are one row, nine or more are two.** Eight in one row are about as tall as the cards in the hand's strip, and nine or ten in one row are too small to read (A413).

**The hand is fanned as it is in play, and the card under the cursor comes forward.** The first build drew the cards upright in a straight row with gaps, and the user asked for it to look like the hand while playing. Each row now lies along an arc: every card is turned a little more than the one before it, about the middle of its bottom edge, and neighbors start slightly less than a card's width apart, so their bottom corners overlap while their names stay clear. Until the inspector exists the overlay is where a card is read, so the card under the cursor glows white, is drawn in front of its neighbors and eases upright; when the cursor leaves, it leans back into the fan.

**The overlay works out which card the cursor is on.** With the cards overlapping and one of them drawn in front, Godot's own picking gives the wrong answer: it goes by the order of the nodes, which drawing a card in front does not change, so the covered corner of the card being read would belong to its neighbor. So the cards ignore the mouse, every part of them, and the overlay takes it: a card is found where it is drawn, turned as it is, the one already under the cursor first and otherwise the one that lies on top. It looks at where the cursor is every frame instead of waiting for the mouse to move, which also finds the card that slides under a resting cursor when the row closes up. This replaced a catcher on each card whose handler ran while its card was being removed, an error Godot printed on every real click and never in a test that called the handler directly; with no handler on the cards that cannot happen. The overlay's clicks and its hover are tested through the real input pipeline.

**The cards move, and the move is remembered outside them.** At first the only motion was a new hand fading in as one, and a card put down vanished while the row snapped shut. Now a new hand is dealt from the Hero's library a card at a time, each turning face up on the way; after a mulligan the old hand flies back first, so the mulligan is seen to happen (A409); a card put on the bottom slides to the library, turning face down, while the row eases closed; the card under the cursor also rises a tenth of its height and grows by 6%, since at eight cards they are small; and when the pregame ends the cards fly to their places in the hand's strip, shrinking to its size, and the strip shows none of them until the overlay has gone. The overlay still rebuilds its cards on every redraw, so the moves are kept in `OpeningMotion`, a pure class the overlay holds outside its children, as Face Motion is kept for the board (ADR 0027): after a redraw it is told where each card belongs, and the overlay places every card each frame from where its move has got to. A redraw in the middle of a deal therefore restarts nothing. A flight is fastest at its start and end and slowest in the middle, the other way round from a face on the board: an inverse sine curve, so a card leaves at speed and arrives at speed. The user asked for it after seeing the first build, which eased in and out, and then for the whole curve after seeing it at half strength. The whole curve leaves and arrives at a speed without limit, so a card was seen to jump at both ends of its flight; the curve now has its two ends cut off (`SINE_REACH`), which leaves a card about two and a half times as fast at its ends as a straight flight and about three times as fast as in its middle. A card in the air is turned exactly as its flight has it, so it lands already turned into the fan; only the card under the cursor eases its turn. While any card is in the air the cursor is on no card and a click chooses none, so a card can never be picked from under a flight, at the cost of putting cards down no faster than one a flight. The buttons wait for nothing: they can be pressed while cards are still in the air. A flight's time goes with its distance, as the user asked after seeing every flight take the same time: a card takes 0.3 seconds at the Fast Game Speed to go the full distance, seven tenths of the overlay's width, which is about as far as the library is from the far end of a full row, and a shorter trip takes a share of that in proportion, never less than four tenths of it, so the row closing over a card put down is quick but is still seen to move. A new hand after a mulligan waits only as long as the old cards take to go back. The cards of a deal leave 0.06 seconds apart at Fast; both times are in `Pacing`, and a screen that is not paced moves nothing. Only the Hero's cards move; the Villain's hand is not on the overlay.

**The buttons are painted, not styled.** Godot's flat style box rounds corners and casts a shadow but cannot shade, and its textured one shades but has square corners, so `ButtonShading` paints a strip once for each color and height (lit from above, with rounded ends, a lighter rim and a soft shadow under it) and stretches only its middle, which works because the light changes from top to bottom alone. Keep is blue and Mulligan burnt orange as before; playing first and drawing are one blue-gray, since neither is the safer choice; Confirm is green, and gray while cards are still owed. A button is lighter under the cursor and darker held down and never changes size: the user turned down a grow on hover.

**The heading stands on a plate, and play or draw is one group.** The heading was plain white text on the scrim, which the user found too slight for the line that says what is being asked. It is now bold, on a dark gray rounded plate in the color of the encounter's windows, with no border; the plate hugs the words, 32 pixels clear at the sides and 12 above and below, so it stays a title at every length, and a frame with no heading has no plate (A562). The line under the heading and the Villain's line are as they were, outside it. While play or draw is chosen there are no cards, and the heading and the two buttons used to stand far apart, where the hand's top and middle would be. They are now one group: the plate above the buttons, 40 pixels between them, and the middle of that space at the middle of the window (A563). On the frames with cards the heading is where it was, above the fan. The scrim is 80% opaque, down from 88%, so a little more of the Backdrop shows through (A561, ADR 0035).

Two alternatives were rejected. Choosing the cards to put down before pressing keep, in one frame, would have had the screen remember them and answer the engine's later question itself; the user preferred the engine's own order, keep and then bottom. Putting cards on the bottom in the hand's strip, as discarding is done, was the roadmap's first wording, but bottoming happens only in the pregame, while the overlay is already up and holds the cards.

# A price is worked out from Rarity by one formula, and the trades it leaves open are accepted

Everything the Hero buys, sells or pays for in a run is priced from one short list by one formula, rather than each good carrying a price of its own. The numbers are tunable constants and live in the code and the roadmap (milestones 24 and 25). What is written here is the shape, and the trades it allows that would otherwise look like mistakes to fix.

**One price for each kind and Rarity.** Cards, Potions and Mods each have a price at Common, Uncommon, Rare and Mythic. An Armor has no Rarity, so each Armor carries its own price. A Power-9 card and a Unique Mod are never for sale, so neither has a buying price. There is no random variance and no sale, so a player learns that a Rare costs the same everywhere. Prices stay flat across the Tiers: a later Tier raises the Rarity of what is stocked, not what a Rarity costs, and the Spoils' gold rises with the Tier instead. So later Stages feel richer, and what is left at the end of a won run goes to the Vault. Prices that rise with the Tier, as the Spoils' gold does, were rejected: every Stage would shop alike, and the list would no longer be learnable.

**A card is priced as it is.** A card's Store price starts from its Rarity's price. Each Hex on it takes 40% off what is left, so the discounts compound, and only then is half of each Mod's Workshop price added. The Hexes come first so that a Hex discounts the card and never the Mod on it. Only half of a Mod's price is added because the Mod is already on a card the Hero did not choose, and choosing which card a Mod goes on is more than half of what the Mod is worth. Paying a Mod's whole price would also make no difference between a Mod on a card and a Mod in the Satchel. The result is rounded to the nearest 5 and is never below 5.

**Everything else follows from that one price.** A shop buys at a third of what it sells for, rounded down: a third rather than half, so that selling middling cards is not worth doing for its own sake. A card is sold for a third of its Store price as it is, so its Mods go with it and are lost; a Hero who wants them back pays for a Removal first, and a Mod that came back for free with a sale would make the Store a cheaper Workshop. A Duplication costs the card's Store price as it is, since a Workshop takes the place of a Store visit most of the time and duplicating a card is buying it. A Removal is one flat price whatever is removed, a Mod or a Hex, of any Rarity: a removed Mod goes back into the Satchel, so the price buys some value back, and a Hex costs the same though it gives nothing back.

**The trades it leaves open.** Removal is cheap enough to allow two trades:
- A Mythic card with one Hex, bought at its discount and then rid of the Hex, comes to the plain card's price.
- A card in stock that carries a Rare or Mythic Mod, bought at the card's price plus half the Mod's and then stripped of the Mod, gets that Mod for less than the Workshop asks, with the card besides. A Duplication of a Modded card followed by a Removal does the same.

Both are accepted. Paying to lift a Hex off a Mythic card costs about a shop visit's gold, and a Hero who finds a Mythic Mod on a card in stock, and can afford both the card and the Removal, has earned the Mod. Raising the Removal price to at least half the dearest Mod's price, which closes both trades, was considered and rejected.

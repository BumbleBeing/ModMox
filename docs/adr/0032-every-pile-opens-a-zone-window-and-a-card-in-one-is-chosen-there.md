# Every pile opens a Zone Window, and a card in one is chosen there

Until 13f a graveyard or an exile was a pile showing its top card and a count, and a library a card back and a count; only the stack, a replacement choice and each player's Emblems had a Zone Window. Now every pile opens one: both graveyards, both exiles and both libraries (A437). 13f was built in one piece, not split into sub-milestones (A436), so it also brought the first card that targets a card in a graveyard (Raise Dead, A442), the first that counters an ability (Stifle, A444), and names for the players (A445). Activating anything from inside a window was left out: an Emblem's click belongs to milestone 14, and no card has an ability that works from a graveyard (A441, ADR 0001).

**A click on a pile opens its window, and a second closes it.** An empty pile opens an empty window (A439). Several may be open at once; each opens to the left of its pile the first time, a little further in and higher than the one opened before so that two never open exactly over each other, and afterward reopens where it was left, as the Emblems windows do. A pile is clicked on its card and its label, through a `ClickCatcher` like everything else (ADR 0022).

**A graveyard or exile is a grid, and a library is one row.** A graveyard's and an exile's cards are Card Faces at the hand's height in a grid that wraps to the window's width, the newest card first (A438). A library is one row scrolled sideways, its top card on the left, with a card back for each card the Hero does not know and its face for one they do (A446, A453). A list of what is left in the Hero's deck was considered and left out: it is a deck tracker, another feature. Both are a `PileWindow`, the shared `ZoneWindow` shell with one of two arrangements.

**The engine remembers who knows a card in a library** (A452). The Player View used to hold only each library's size. Each card in a library now records which players know it: a card put there from a hand is known to the player who held it, and a shuffle of that library forgets everything in it. So far only the pregame puts a card into a library, on the bottom after a mulligan; a card put there from a zone everyone sees will be known to everyone when a card does that. A view lists each library top first, a known card as itself and any other as null, so the Villain's view never holds what only the Hero knows. The Villain's Agent does not yet use what it knows of its own library: its Simulation Copies re-deal the library as before (A454, ADR 0020).

**A window is titled with its player's name** (A438): "Jackson's Graveyard (5)". The Emblems windows too. Each Emblem's row now shows its generated rules text on one line, cut with an ellipsis where the window ends, in place of "Triggered ability"; widening the window shows more (A440, ADR 0023).

**Players have names, and everything that names one reads them from the snapshot** (A445). No player had a name: the panels, stack entries and replacement text all said "Hero" and "Villain" from one function. An encounter request now carries a name for each, from the start form or `--hero-name=` and `--villain-name=`, the presenter hands them on in every snapshot as a `PlayerNames`, and a player given none goes by their part. A part takes "the" in a sentence and a name does not: "The Villain is thinking…" but "Mishra is thinking…". The roguelike around the encounter will supply the names later. One sentence is unchanged: the presenter's refusal while the Villain's Agent runs still reads "The Villain is thinking.", because it is a constant many callers compare against.

**A target requirement says where its targets are, and has two more places** (A448, engine ADR 0014). Cards in graveyards, which Raise Dead narrows with a Filter to creature cards its controller owns (`OwnedByFilter`, since a card in a graveyard has an owner and no controller), and abilities on the stack, activated or triggered and either player's. A mana ability never uses the stack, so it is never one. A countered ability simply leaves the stack, and the event says it was countered. `ReturnToHandEffect` puts a card from a graveyard into its owner's hand; returning a permanent waits for a card that does it.

**A card in a graveyard is chosen in its window, and the pile glows** (A443). `DecisionClicks` says which piles hold a card that answers (`piles()`) and whether every answer is in a pile (`is_only_in_piles()`). A pile that holds an answer glows as a target does. When nothing on the board answers, as with Raise Dead, the window opens by itself. When a permanent or a player answers too, nothing opens until the Hero clicks the glowing pile, because they may mean the permanent. No card in the game offers that choice yet, so its test shows the screen a made-up decision. In the window the cards that answer glow and take the click, and the others take none.

**A window opened to answer a target closes with the answer** (A450). Whether the game opened it or the Hero did by clicking the glowing pile, it closes once the target is chosen or the cast is canceled. One the Hero already had open stays open, and one the Hero closes while still asked is not opened again for that decision. This is kept in `PileWindows`, a pure class the screen holds outside what it redraws, as it holds a `CombatSelection`.

**An ability on the stack is targeted on its entry** (A449). Nothing was needed: the Stack Window already made any entry that answers a target glow and take a click, and drew an arrow from a spell to whatever on the stack it targets. Tests now cover an ability's entry. A target in a graveyard is named on the spell's entry, "Hero → Scathe Zombies", and marks nothing on the board.

**A closed window costs a redraw nothing.** The board is rebuilt from every snapshot (ADR 0027), and six windows of up to forty faces each would have been rebuilt with it. A pile window is filled when it opens, and while open only when what it shows has changed: which cards, in what order, and which of them answer.

**A card leaving a graveyard flies from its pile**, with the window open or shut (ADR 0027). The window shows a zone; the pile is where the zone is.

**The decks** (A447): two Raise Dead replace a Pearled Unicorn and a Scathe Zombies in `white_black`, and two Stifle replace the Gray Ogre and a Wall of Stone in `blue_red`. Stifle is not an Alpha card, so it is the first card in a second set's folder, `content/cards/scourge/`.

**The Villain's Agent** (A451). A full Agent weighs both cards by simulation like any other play; countering an ability carries the counter Timing Tag, so it is considered only with the other player's spell or ability on top of the stack. A cheap Agent casts Raise Dead in its own main phase at the creature card it values most, by the rule it already used for its own creatures, and never counters an ability, because what an ability would do cannot be read from a cost. It was also stopped from casting an ability counter at a spell.

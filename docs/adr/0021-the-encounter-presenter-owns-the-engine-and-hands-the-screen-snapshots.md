# The Encounter Presenter owns the engine and hands the screen snapshots

All GUI code is game-side, under `ui/`, and only one object in it touches the rules engine. The Encounter Presenter (`ui/encounter/encounter_presenter.gd`) owns the `MmEngine` and the `MmEncounterDriver`. After every change it hands the Encounter Screen a snapshot: the Hero's Player View, the Pending Decision the Hero must answer (or none), whether the Villain is thinking, whether the Hero is holding priority, whether anything can be undone, the Event Log entries added since the previous snapshot, and a `CardLook` for every Game Object the view shows. The screen draws from the snapshot and nothing else. A test scans `ui/` and `tools/` and fails if any file but the presenter mentions `MmEngine`, `MmGameState`, `MmEncounterDriver` or `game_state(`. This is ADR 0005's boundary carried into the GUI: the screen cannot show the Hero a card they may not see, because it is never handed one.

**The worker thread.** A Normal AI decision can take over a second (ADR 0020), so the Villain's Agent never runs on the main thread. The presenter runs `driver.run()` on a `WorkerThreadPool` task with `decision_cap = 1`, so each task gives exactly one Villain answer and the screen is told after every one (A238). While a task runs, the worker is changing the game, so the main thread must not read the engine at all. The presenter therefore makes the snapshot *before* it starts the task, flags that snapshot as "the Villain is thinking", and refuses every command until the task has been collected. A redraw in the middle of a thought, such as when the window is resized, uses the snapshot it already has. The prototype got this order wrong once and built a Player View while the worker was recalculating layers, which crashed on a half-computed permanent; that is the failure this rule prevents. Leaving the screen waits for a running task first.

**Art.** A Player View names a card by its Card Identity. `MmEngine.run_card(card_id)` leads from that back to the Run Card, and the presenter takes the art key from its Card Definition's file name. An ability on the stack takes its source's art. The alternative, rebuilding the engine's numbering from the setup's deck order inside the GUI, would have copied an engine rule and broken silently if it changed (A241).

**Events.** A snapshot carries only the events since the one before, for the animations of 13d. A rewind (undo, or canceling a cast) only shortens the log, so it hands out nothing and is never animated (ADR 0015).

**Passing for the Hero.** The presenter passes priority on the Hero's behalf in two cases, both decided from the Hero's Player View alone.

- *Auto-pass*, on by default. The Hero is asked only on a step that has a stop and where they could cast, play or activate something other than a mana ability, or when the Villain's spell or ability is on top of the stack. Each player's turn has its own row of stops, held in `StepStops`; untap and cleanup never hold one. Clearing the stop the Hero is waiting at lets auto-pass carry on at once.
- *The Hero's own spell or ability on top of the stack* is passed whether auto-pass is on or not, unless the hold key (Ctrl) is down at the moment it goes there. The hold is latched until the Hero's next answer, so letting go of the key does not pass, and it is decided again each time one of the Hero's objects comes to the top.

**Pacing is not here yet.** The Villain answers as fast as it can. A minimum of about 600 ms between Villain actions, and a third of that for tapping lands, is agreed for 13d, where it belongs with the animations (A238, A239).

**The screen.** `EncounterLayout` works out every Region's rectangle from the screen's size, so "no two Regions overlap" is tested without a window. The game is designed at 1920 × 1080 and stretched with `canvas_items` and the `expand` aspect: rows keep their share of the height, the sidebar and the pile column keep their width, and extra width goes to the battlefield (A205). Each player's pile column runs library, graveyard, exile from the top. The Hero's controls (undo, pass and auto-pass) fill a section of the Hero's panel in the sidebar, under the Emblems button, which leaves the pile column to the piles and, between the two players' piles, the line saying the Villain is thinking or the Hero is holding priority. The Hero's hand sits a quarter of a card below the bottom of the screen, cut off a little under its type lines. The Hero's rows sit that much lower with it while the Villain's stay up by their hand, which opens a 90 px lane across the middle of the board for combat. A player's nonland row is two lines: creatures toward the left and middle, and other permanents to their right at 85% of the size, set back toward that player's edge and taking at most a third of the row (A276, A277). A line keeps its faces' size and scrolls sideways when it overflows, with an arrow at each end it can scroll toward and a thin bar beneath; land rows shrink their faces instead (A278). Once attackers are declared, creatures not in the combat are drawn a fifth smaller, and attackers and blockers step 40 px toward the middle, into the lane between the two players' rows (A279). The stack is not a Region. It is the Stack Window, which is there exactly while something is on the stack. Every zone's window shares one shell, `ZoneWindow`: draggable and resizable within the battlefield, scrolling on overflow, titled with the zone's name and count, keeping its place and size while hidden, and closable except for the Stack Window.

**Decisions get their own inputs, one kind at a time (A243).** Choosing from a list is not fun, so every decision kind will have an input of its own: glowing targets to click, mana sources to tap, cards to drag. Until a kind has one, the Debug Decision Panel answers it through one of five generic forms (`DebugDecisionForm`): pick one, pick some, pair up, put in order, split a number. A kind is not finished until its panel form is deleted. The Glow, which marks what a decision can choose (orange-red for targets, blue for mana sources, green otherwise), is built now as display only and is the seam those inputs will click through. A Glow, and any other effect on a thing's border, is a child of the Control it marks, so it turns and resizes with it.

Two alternatives were rejected. Letting each screen node read the engine directly is simpler to write, but it is how the crash above happened, and it makes "what may the Hero see" a property of every node instead of one object. Running the whole Villain turn in a single task would save a few thread handoffs, but the screen would only see where the turn ended up, with nothing to draw in between.

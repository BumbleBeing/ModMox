# The presenter pays the Hero's mana, and shows beforehand what it would tap

Until 13e-3 the Hero paid every cost by hand: click each glowing source, or a mana button, until nothing was owed. ADR 0022 put paying automatically off until clicking felt right and there was a rule for which lands to keep. Now there is Auto-tap: when a cast reaches payment, the Encounter Presenter answers the engine's payment decisions for the Hero, and the Hero is never shown them. It is on by default, turned off with the Auto-tapping box on the Pause Menu's settings page, and kept in the settings file (A414, A426).

**It pays at once, with no extra click.** A Pay button that finished a payment, and a preview the Hero confirmed or edited, were both considered and rejected: each adds a click to every spell to guard a rare case, in a game whose only mana sources are basic lands, Llanowar Elves and Dark Ritual (A414). The Hero who wants particular lands tapped taps them first, at priority, as they always could: mana in the pool is spent before anything is tapped.

**The presenter answers, as it passes priority** (A415, ADR 0021). It already answers for the Hero in its `_advance()` loop when it passes priority and when it orders triggers that are all alike, and it is the only object that may read the engine. Every `PayManaDecision` the Hero is asked is covered, a spell's or an activated ability's, because the engine asks for both the same way and a split would be a rule to learn (A418). Only payment is answered: targets are still the Hero's, and are chosen before anything is tapped.

**It chooses as the Villain does** (A416). `AiPaymentPlanner` is asked for each step: pool mana first, colored symbols from sources of that color, generic mana from the source whose color the rest of the hand needs least, and a land before another source before a creature. It reads the Hero's own hand and permanents and nothing hidden. One gap in it was closed for both players: for a colored symbol it went by demand and age alone, so it could tap Llanowar Elves for {G} while a Forest stood untapped; the kind of source now comes before both (A417).

**Who pays is settled once, when the cast reaches payment.** By hand if Auto-tap is off or the Hold Key is down at that moment, and then by hand for all of that payment: letting go of the key partway, or turning the setting on in the Pause Menu, does not make the game tap the rest (A419, A420). A live key would tap the Hero's remaining lands the instant a finger slipped. The Hold Key was chosen over a new key because it already means "do nothing for me". A payment that undo leads back to is also the Hero's, always, so that undo never has the step it took back paid again under it. In practice undo rarely meets an automatic payment: the Hero's own spell is passed as soon as it is cast, which is an undo barrier, unless the Hold Key went down while the taps were being shown.

**Each tap is shown, as the Villain's are** (A425). The first payment is made the instant the cast reaches payment and the last one casts the spell with no wait after it; between two payments the snapshot stays up for the mana wait, scaled by the Game Speed, so a three-land spell takes two waits (A427). Paying everything in one snapshot was the first proposal and the user preferred the taps spaced. A presenter that is not paced, as in a test, pays everything at once. While a payment is being shown the snapshot asks the Hero nothing; it carries the payment decision being answered and what is still to be tapped (`paying`, `payment_left`). The Info Panel reads "Paying for Hill Giant" with what is still owed, and its Cancel button works: canceling takes the whole cast back and is the one thing the Hero can do meanwhile (A428). Answers, passing and undo are refused, and a setting changed meanwhile does not hurry the next tap. The sources still to be tapped keep their blue Glow until their turn, so the payment reads as the Tap Preview being carried out (A429).

**The Tap Preview shows what would be tapped before the Hero commits.** While the cursor is on something that costs mana, only the sources Auto-tap would tap for it keep their blue Glow, and the pool mana it would spend glows on its mana button; every other source stops glowing, though it can still be tapped by hand (A415). It shows for a card of the hand under the cursor, for a card held, through the whole drag, and for a permanent whose one ability costs mana (A421). Something that offers several things shows nothing until the Option Menu is open, and then the payment of the row the cursor is on (A418). A thing with no mana cost changes no Glow, or sweeping the cursor along a hand of lands would make the board blink (A422). There is no preview with Auto-tap off or the Hold Key down, since it would mark lands the game is not going to tap (A423). It reuses the Glow's blue: only the blue is taken from a source the plan leaves alone, so a permanent with another ability stays purple (A424).

**The preview is the payment.** A plan is not a second rule. The presenter makes a Simulation Copy of the game with every card where it is, starts the cast on it, takes the first target offered (what a spell is aimed at changes nothing about its cost), and pays with the same planner, noting each source and pool unit: a `PaymentPlan`. A test casts dozens of spells from the real decks and checks that what was planned is what was tapped.

**A plan is worked out when it is asked for.** The first build put a plan for every castable thing into each snapshot. Paying on a copy costs about a millisecond for each mana, and with eight things to cast that was 10 ms on average and 33 ms at worst, at every priority, which is a frame or two. So the screen asks `payment_plan(answer)` for what the cursor is on, the presenter works it out then (under 5 ms) and keeps it until the next snapshot. This is the one thing the screen reads that is not in the snapshot. The presenter guards the worker thread itself: it gives no plan while the Villain is thinking.

**Nothing is rebuilt for the preview.** The screen asks the cursor each frame what it wants previewed (`follow_preview()`), and when that changes it restyles the Glows of the faces that exist, which also keeps it within the rule for a held card (ADR 0022). The previewed answer is kept outside the redrawn nodes, so a redraw draws the preview again; a new snapshot forgets it, because an answer's number means something else there, and asks again at once. What the cursor is on is worked out from where the faces are drawn, since Godot has not yet said what the mouse is over on a board just rebuilt.

**A presenter pays nothing until told to.** Auto-tap is off in a presenter until the screen turns it on from the settings, as Pacing is, so the tests of paying by hand, and every test that casts through a presenter, play as they did.

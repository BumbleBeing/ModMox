# A Mod is a game-side item that wraps engine Modifications

A Mod is a resource on the game's side of the boundary. It holds a name, whether it is a Hex, a Rarity, an Augmentation and a Mod Cost, the card types it accepts, and whether it is once per card. The Augmentation is one or more engine Modifications, and the Mod Cost one Modification that adds a mana cost to the card's, its generic mana to the card's generic mana (`AddManaCostModification`), or none for a Mod that accepts lands. Additional costs will join the Mod Cost once the engine casts with them. Applying a Mod appends the Augmentation's Modifications and then the Mod Cost's to the Run Card (ADR 0004), and the engine never learns a Mod was involved. Keeping the two apart lets the game show and price them separately; to the engine both are just Modifications.

**A Mod's text is written from its Modifications** by `ModText`, the way rules text is written from abilities (ADR 0023), wherever a Mod is shown off a card: in the Modify Window, the Workshop and the Spoils. It has no hand-written description, so its text cannot disagree with what it does.

Every card has three Mod Slots, Hexes included. A basic land has them too, and its first Mod makes it a Run Card of the Collection's (A558, ADR 0028). A Mod is applicable to a card when the card has an empty Mod Slot and has any of the Mod's card types as it reads now, after the Mods already on it, and when any rule of the Mod's own also accepts it, such as needing a mana cost. A Mod that accepts Land has no Mod Cost, whatever else it accepts, because a land is never cast and a Mod Cost would add nothing to it; every other Mod has one. Amended in the Store's design round: a Mod that accepted Land once accepted no other type. An Omni Mod accepts every card type, lands included, such as one that gives cycling, an ability used from the hand that adds nothing to a mana cost, so its Mod Cost is none on any card. `Mod.problems()` checks a Mod's shape: this, a name, an Augmentation, a card type, a Mod Cost for any other Mod, and that a Unique Mod is once per card and never a Hex. The content tests will run it over every authored Mod once there are any. The same Mod may go on a card more than once, each copy adding its whole Augmentation and Mod Cost, unless it is once per card, as every Unique Mod is. The same rules hold for every card, with no list of their own for basic lands. Both checks are made game-side. The engine's `MmRunCard` will flatten any number of Modifications and does not know the limit.

**A Mod's name goes in front of the card's** (A559). A Grizzly Bears with a Mod named Violent is named Violent Grizzly Bears, and a Plains with one named Ancient is Ancient Plains. The names of the Mods and Hexes on the card come in alphabetical order, then the card's own name, whose capitalization is never changed. A Mod on the card twice is named once with Very in front, three times with Extremely, and Very and Extremely play no part in the order: Ancient Very Violent Plains. Alphabetical order was chosen over slot order so a card's name does not depend on the order its Mods were applied; the Modifications themselves still apply in that order. The name is built game-side from the Mods on the card (`ModdedName`), and no engine Modification renames the card for it. A rule that asks for a name reads the Original's (ADR 0004).

**Mods are applied only in the deckbuilder** (A544). The Workshop and Spoils hand Mods out, and the Workshop removes them: a removed Mod goes back into the Satchel and a removed Hex is gone (ADR 0070). But there is one place, one Modify Window and one "are you sure" for putting a Mod on a card.

A Hex is a Mod like any other in data, but the Hero never holds or applies one. It arrives already on a card and takes a Mod Slot: a card in a Store's stock may carry one, at a discount, and so may a card a Chaos Node event touches (ADR 0071). Its Augmentation may be a downside, such as "this enters tapped". No Hex is Unique.

**Unique is a Mod's fifth Rarity**, where a card has Power-9. A Unique Mod is won only from a Unique Villain or a rare Chaos Node event: no other Spoils hold one and no Workshop sells one, though a Workshop buys and removes one like any other.

The alternative was to ask the engine's Modification whether it can apply to a card, since it knows what it changes. It was rejected because which cards may take an item, how many a card may carry, and whether an item is a Hex are rules of the roguelike, not of Magic, and the engine never refers to the game. Keeping them game-side also lets two Mods share one Modification and differ only in what they accept.

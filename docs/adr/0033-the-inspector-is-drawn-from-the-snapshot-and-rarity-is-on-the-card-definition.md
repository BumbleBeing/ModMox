# The Inspector is drawn from the snapshot, and Rarity is on the Card Definition

Until 13h a card could be read only where it was drawn: small on the battlefield, fanned in the hand, one line in a window. Now a right-click on a card pins it, and the Inspector takes the sidebar's place and shows it in full: its Card Face as wide as the sidebar, a View Original button under it, its State List and its Reminders (A455). It was prototyped first, starting from the deckbuilder prototype's inspector, and built after the user signed off on it.

**Only a right-click shows it** (A472). The prototype and the first build also showed whatever card the cursor was on, at once, and held it for a moment after the cursor left so that crossing the gap between two cards did not flash the sidebar (A458). Played, that was too busy: mousing over the hand swapped the sidebar for a card and back over and over. Hovering now shows nothing. The roadmap's "replaces the sidebar while a card is hovered" is given up for this.

**Which card is pinned is kept in a pure class, and found from where things are drawn.** `Inspection` holds the Pinned card. The Encounter Screen keeps one outside everything a redraw replaces and tells it of right-clicks (`right_click_at()`), working out the card under the click itself from where the faces and window entries are (`inspectable_at()`). No face takes the mouse for this, so ADR 0022 stands as it was: only what answers a decision has a `ClickCatcher`. A card the Hero may not see cannot be pinned because it is drawn as a back with no Game Object behind it (ADR 0021).

**A right-click pins, and almost anything lets go.** A right-click on another card pins that one instead. A second right-click on the Pinned card, a right-click on empty space, Escape, or a left-click anywhere but on the Inspector lets it go (A459). It has no close button: one was built, and was dropped for a line of tiny text at the top, "[Click off to close]", with four pixels of room above and below it, which let the card sit higher (A473, A474). That left-click then does whatever it does, so nothing costs two clicks (A461). Escape opens the Pause Menu only when nothing is pinned. The Inspector covers the whole sidebar and Space, Z and Ctrl go on working (A462); keeping the life totals in view above it was considered and left until it is missed.

**Nothing is pinned while anything is dragged** (A460). A right-click while a card or a trigger is held puts it down and pins nothing. A pin never survives into a drag, because the press that starts one is a left-click outside the Inspector (A467), so the player panels are there for the whole of a drag.

**Every place a known card is drawn can be pinned from** (A463, A469): the battlefield, the hand, an entry of the Stack Window, an Emblem in its window, a face-up card in a pile's window, and a card of the opening hand through the pregame, though none while cards are in the air there and may be face down. An ability on the stack is read as the card it came from: as that card is now while it can still be seen, and otherwise as its Original. This amends ADR 0030, which kept the sidebar readable through the pregame: it now gives way to the Inspector while a card is pinned, as it does in play.

**The Original is the Card Definition** (A465): no Mods, no Hexes and nothing the encounter has done. The Run Card as it entered the encounter was the other candidate; the Card Definition was chosen because the question View Original answers is "what was this before anything changed it". A token or an Emblem has none, and the button cannot be pressed for it. It is a real toggle button, centered under the card (A470), and it is let up whenever another card is shown.

**The State List is made by the presenter and drawn blind** (A456, A457). `StateList` turns what a Player View says of a permanent into entries of a title and a sentence: summoning sickness, damage marked, each continuous effect with the card it came from, and each shield. The presenter puts one on the snapshot for every permanent that has anything to say, and the Inspector draws whatever it is handed without knowing what kinds exist. Counters and attachments will add entries there when the game has them (ADR 0001), and nothing in the Inspector will change. Tapped is left out because the board shows it. The engine still produces only facts; the words are game-side, as rules text is (ADR 0023).

**Reminders are a table beside the rules text, one for each keyword that has an icon** (A464). `Reminders` gives one for each keyword a card has, in the order of its Keyword Chips, and a test fails for a Keyword Icon with no Reminder. They are written for plain labels, so none uses a mana or tap symbol. Terms that are not keywords, such as "counter" or "regenerate", have none yet. The State List and the Reminders each have a scrolling section of their own, no taller than what is in it and capped, so neither can push the other off the Inspector.

**Rarity is a field on the Card Definition** (A471). It is Common, Uncommon, Rare, Mythic or Power-9. No rule reads it, but it is part of a card's base design and the deckbuilder's filter wants it as much as the Inspector does, so it lives with the rest of the design in the card's file. A game-side table keyed by card was the alternative: it would have kept the engine's class untouched and split a card's design across two places. The cards were given the rarity of their first printing, with Ancestral Recall as Power-9; nothing is Mythic yet. The presenter puts each card's Rarity on the snapshot beside its Original, and the Inspector glows round the face in its color: gray, silver, gold, honey and light green (A466). The glow is on the Inspector's face only. The card on the board gets no rarity glow, since a Glow there already means "this answers a decision" (ADR 0022).

**The Pinned card wears a ring that is part of the card** (A470). `BorderEffects.add_pin()` makes it a child of the face or entry, as every border effect is, so a card lying over the Pinned card in the hand covers its ring too. The prototype first drew the ring over the screen from the card's rectangle, and it showed on top of the neighbors.

**It eases, and follows Game Speed** (A468). The Inspector fades in and out over `Pacing.EASE_SECONDS`, as windows and Glows do. A change of card takes one ease in all: the old card fades out for half of it and the new one in for the rest. The same card, changed by a new snapshot or by View Original, is redrawn at once. A screen that is not paced, as in tests, has no fade.

**Since milestone 14 a Potion and an Emblem are read in the Inspector as themselves, not as cards** (A532, A533). Both are pinned by the same right-click as a card, from their window's entry. Neither has an Original, a State List or Reminders, so those are left out.

**A Potion is shown as its Potion Icon, enlarged.** The icon stands where the Card Face would, scaled up with hard pixel edges and glowing in its Rarity's color, with the Potion's name, a type line that reads "Potion" and its rules text under it. A Potion has a Rarity because it is an Item that is bought, sold and won; it is the same field on the Card Definition that a card's is (A524). A Potion Icon is a 16 × 16 pixel icon in `ui/sprites/potions/`, named after the Potion's definition file and built by `tools/sprites/build.sh`; a Potion without one gets a plain bottle (A530).

**An Emblem is shown on an Emblem Face.** It is the size of a Card Face, so the Inspector's layout does not change, and has what an Emblem has: its art, its name, a type line that reads "Emblem", its rules text with any spent ability gray and marked "(used)", and the artist's credit along the bottom. A Card Face would have left an empty mana cost and power/toughness box. It has no Rarity glow, because an Emblem comes from a Status and is not an Item (A535). Its border is its own; for now that border is a plain gray frame and the art a gray box, until the user supplies both.

**The type lines are the GUI's** (A536). The engine has no "Emblem" or "Potion" card type (engine ADRs 0011 and 0036): the presenter knows which each object is from the list it is on in the Player View, and the face writes the word.

**Emblem art is looked up as card art is** (A534). The Boons are definition files in `content/emblems/`. Their art goes in `content/art/emblems/`, named after the file, with a manifest there that holds each picture's artist and focus point, read by the same `ArtLibrary` code. An Emblem without art shows the gray box and no credit. A sample Emblem made from a card's abilities borrows that card's art and credit.

**A Potion Icon is always drawn on beige with a darker circle behind it** (A538, from the signed-off prototype). The square is the palette's lightest earth, `#e6dcbd`, and the circle `#a8926f`, drawn in the icon's own pixels so that its edge steps as the icon's does. `PotionArt` draws all of it. The circle always fills the icon's 16 × 16 grid. In the Inspector the icon has a margin of one art pixel of beige; in the small art, on an entry of the Potions window or the Stack Window, the icon fills the square and so does the circle. The prototype's small circle was one art pixel wider all round and one lower, cut off by the square; in the game an entry's row can be taller than the square, the circle showed below it, and the user had it shrunk to fit.

**A look says which kind of thing it is** (milestone 14). `CardLook.kind` is a card, an Emblem or a Potion, set by the presenter with `CardLook.of_held()`, which also writes the text in the thing's own words ("this emblem", "this potion"), one ability to an entry, with which of them are Spent. The Inspector draws a `CardFace`, an `EmblemFace` or a `PotionReadout` from it and is handed nothing new. An ability on the stack that came from a Potion has a Potion's look, made from what the Potion was authored as, since an ability on the stack has no abilities of its own to read: its entry keeps the Potion's art, and pinned it reads as the Potion did, by the Potion's name.

**The button reads View Original and the keyword section is titled Reminders, in the encounter and in the deckbuilder** (A553). They were See Original and "Keywords" until milestone 15, when the deckbuilder's inspector was given the same two parts and one name was chosen for each. The deckbuilder's inspector is laid out as bordered, titled panels under the face: Card Text and Mods, with one Reminders panel taking their place while a Reminders button beside View Original is pressed (roadmap item 15). The encounter's Inspector is to be given the same layout, with its State List where the Mods panel is, as a later GUI step of its own (A556). The deckbuilder also takes the encounter's easing: its inspector and windows use `Easing`, half again as slowly (A565).

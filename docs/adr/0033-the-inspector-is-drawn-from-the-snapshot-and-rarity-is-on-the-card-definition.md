# The Inspector is drawn from the snapshot, and Rarity is on the Card Definition

Until 13h a card could be read only where it was drawn: small on the battlefield, fanned in the hand, one line in a window. Now a right-click on a card pins it, and the Inspector takes the sidebar's place and shows it in full: its Card Face as wide as the sidebar, a See Original button under it, its State List and its Reminders (A455). It was prototyped first, starting from the deckbuilder prototype's inspector, and built after the user signed off on it.

**Only a right-click shows it** (A472). The prototype and the first build also showed whatever card the cursor was on, at once, and held it for a moment after the cursor left so that crossing the gap between two cards did not flash the sidebar (A458). Played, that was too busy: mousing over the hand swapped the sidebar for a card and back over and over. Hovering now shows nothing. The roadmap's "replaces the sidebar while a card is hovered" is given up for this.

**Which card is pinned is kept in a pure class, and found from where things are drawn.** `Inspection` holds the Pinned card. The Encounter Screen keeps one outside everything a redraw replaces and tells it of right-clicks (`right_click_at()`), working out the card under the click itself from where the faces and window entries are (`inspectable_at()`). No face takes the mouse for this, so ADR 0022 stands as it was: only what answers a decision has a `ClickCatcher`. A card the Hero may not see cannot be pinned because it is drawn as a back with no Game Object behind it (ADR 0021).

**A right-click pins, and almost anything lets go.** A right-click on another card pins that one instead. A second right-click on the Pinned card, a right-click on empty space, Escape, or a left-click anywhere but on the Inspector lets it go (A459). It has no close button: one was built, and was dropped for a line of tiny text at the top, "[Click off to close]", with four pixels of room above and below it, which let the card sit higher (A473, A474). That left-click then does whatever it does, so nothing costs two clicks (A461). Escape opens the Pause Menu only when nothing is pinned. The Inspector covers the whole sidebar and Space, Z and Ctrl go on working (A462); keeping the life totals in view above it was considered and left until it is missed.

**Nothing is pinned while anything is dragged** (A460). A right-click while a card or a trigger is held puts it down and pins nothing. A pin never survives into a drag, because the press that starts one is a left-click outside the Inspector (A467), so the player panels are there for the whole of a drag.

**Every place a known card is drawn can be pinned from** (A463, A469): the battlefield, the hand, an entry of the Stack Window, an Emblem in its window, a face-up card in a pile's window, and a card of the opening hand through the pregame, though none while cards are in the air there and may be face down. An ability on the stack is read as the card it came from: as that card is now while it can still be seen, and otherwise as its Original. This amends ADR 0030, which kept the sidebar readable through the pregame: it now gives way to the Inspector while a card is pinned, as it does in play.

**The Original is the Card Definition** (A465): no Mods, no Hexes and nothing the encounter has done. The Run Card as it entered the encounter was the other candidate; the Card Definition was chosen because the question See Original answers is "what was this before anything changed it". A token or an Emblem has none, and the button cannot be pressed for it. It is a real toggle button, centered under the card (A470), and it is let up whenever another card is shown.

**The State List is made by the presenter and drawn blind** (A456, A457). `StateList` turns what a Player View says of a permanent into entries of a title and a sentence: summoning sickness, damage marked, each continuous effect with the card it came from, and each shield. The presenter puts one on the snapshot for every permanent that has anything to say, and the Inspector draws whatever it is handed without knowing what kinds exist. Counters and attachments will add entries there when the game has them (ADR 0001), and nothing in the Inspector will change. Tapped is left out because the board shows it. The engine still produces only facts; the words are game-side, as rules text is (ADR 0023).

**Reminders are a table beside the rules text, one for each keyword that has an icon** (A464). `Reminders` gives one for each keyword a card has, in the order of its Keyword Chips, and a test fails for a Keyword Icon with no Reminder. They are written for plain labels, so none uses a mana or tap symbol. Terms that are not keywords, such as "counter" or "regenerate", have none yet. The State List and the Reminders each have a scrolling section of their own, no taller than what is in it and capped, so neither can push the other off the Inspector.

**Rarity is a field on the Card Definition** (A471). It is Common, Uncommon, Rare, Mythic or Power-9. No rule reads it, but it is part of a card's base design and the deckbuilder's filter wants it as much as the Inspector does, so it lives with the rest of the design in the card's file. A game-side table keyed by card was the alternative: it would have kept the engine's class untouched and split a card's design across two places. The cards were given the rarity of their first printing, with Ancestral Recall as Power-9; nothing is Mythic yet. The presenter puts each card's Rarity on the snapshot beside its Original, and the Inspector glows round the face in its color: gray, silver, gold, honey and light green (A466). The glow is on the Inspector's face only. The card on the board gets no rarity glow, since a Glow there already means "this answers a decision" (ADR 0022).

**The Pinned card wears a ring that is part of the card** (A470). `BorderEffects.add_pin()` makes it a child of the face or entry, as every border effect is, so a card lying over the Pinned card in the hand covers its ring too. The prototype first drew the ring over the screen from the card's rectangle, and it showed on top of the neighbors.

**It eases, and follows Game Speed** (A468). The Inspector fades in and out over `Pacing.EASE_SECONDS`, as windows and Glows do. A change of card takes one ease in all: the old card fades out for half of it and the new one in for the rest. The same card, changed by a new snapshot or by See Original, is redrawn at once. A screen that is not paced, as in tests, has no fade.

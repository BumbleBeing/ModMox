# A redrawing screen is animated by remembering where each face was

The Encounter Screen throws its board away and rebuilds it from every snapshot (ADR 0021). That stays. Movement is added on top by Face Motion (`ui/encounter/face_motion.gd`), an object the screen keeps outside the redrawn nodes, as it keeps the `CombatSelection`. Face Motion holds no nodes. It remembers, for each permanent, where its face was last drawn: the middle of the face on the screen, its height, how far it has turned toward tapped and how solid it is (a `FacePose`). After a redraw the screen tells it where every face now belongs, and every frame the screen asks for each face's pose and places the node there. A face whose place, size or turn changed eases from wherever it is to where it belongs over a quarter of a second, slow at both ends. A permanent that is new fades in at its place. One that has left is kept as a ghost, drawn from its last `CardLook` on a layer a redraw leaves alone, and fades out where it was, taking no clicks.

This shape was chosen because it leaves the redraw alone. A redraw in the middle of a move is harmless: the new node is placed where the old one had got to and carries on, and a redraw of the same board changes nothing because the faces already belong where they are going. Input is never held up. What takes a click is a child of the face, so a face is clicked where it is drawn, and a new answer mid-move simply sets off the next move from there. Glows, Stack Marks and steppers are children of the face too and go with it; block lines, which are not, are worked out again each frame while anything moves.

**What does not move.** A rewind (undo, or a canceled cast) and a window of another size put every face at its place at once and leave no ghosts, because neither is something that happened in the game (ADR 0015). The snapshot says whether it came from a rewind, since an empty event list cannot tell a rewind from a quiet pass. Scrolling a line is not a move either: the screen tells Face Motion where the scroll carried each face. A permanent that leaves while scrolled out of sight gets no ghost, which would otherwise appear outside its line. The Hero's own clicks in a half-made combat selection do move things: an attacker eases into the lane when clicked.

**The clock.** Face Motion counts time a frame at a time and never counts a frame as more than a thirtieth of a second. The frame that rebuilds the board is a slow one, and timing a move from the wall clock let that frame eat the start of every move, which read as a jump (the first prototype did this). The curve is slow-fast-slow for the same reason: a curve that starts fast covers a quarter of a turn in its first frame.

**Pacing.** The Villain answers faster than a human can read, so the Encounter Presenter holds back collecting the Villain's next answer until the last snapshot has been up long enough (`ui/encounter/pacing.gd`). The wait comes from what that snapshot showed, never from the kind of answer that made it: nothing for no events or only bookkeeping (a step or turn starting, mana emptying, untapping, combat ending, effects ending, damage wearing off), 270 ms when the rest is only tapping and mana, and 500 ms for anything else. A pass that makes a spell resolve therefore waits as long as casting one. The mana wait is a little longer than a move, so each land finishes turning before the next starts. Because the answer is simply not collected yet, thinking time counts toward the wait, the screen never shows an old game, and the rule that nothing reads the engine while the worker has it is untouched. The wait applies only between two Villain answers: the Hero is asked the moment the Villain's answer is collected, and the Villain's first answer after the Hero's is not held back. Time spent paused counts toward the wait.

The presenter and Face Motion take their numbers as plain values that default to zero, and the screen sets the game's numbers only when it starts a real encounter (`use_game_pace`). Tests, the soak and `--screenshot` therefore never wait and never see a face part-way.

**Not yet.** Cards do not fly between zones: a card that changes zones fades out in one place and in at another. The hand keeps its own easing toward the cursor. There is no setting for speed.

Two alternatives were rejected. Keeping the nodes and changing only what differs between snapshots would animate naturally, but it rewrites the screen and gives up the property that the board is a function of the snapshot alone. Queuing snapshots and playing each event as a scripted animation would let every event have its own flourish, but the screen would then show a game older than the one being decided, and every decision input would have to wait for the queue to drain.

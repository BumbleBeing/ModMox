# A redrawing screen is animated by remembering where each face was

The Encounter Screen throws its board away and rebuilds it from every snapshot (ADR 0021). That stays. Movement is added on top by Face Motion (`ui/encounter/face_motion.gd`), an object the screen keeps outside the redrawn nodes, as it keeps the `CombatSelection`. Face Motion holds no nodes. It remembers, for each permanent, where its face was last drawn: the middle of the face on the screen, its height, how far it has turned toward tapped and how solid it is (a `FacePose`). After a redraw the screen tells it where every face now belongs, and every frame the screen asks for each face's pose and places the node there. A face whose place, size or turn changed eases from wherever it is to where it belongs over a quarter of a second, slow at both ends. A permanent that is new fades in at its place. One that has left is kept as a ghost, drawn from its last `CardLook` on a layer a redraw leaves alone, and fades out where it was, taking no clicks.

This shape was chosen because it leaves the redraw alone. A redraw in the middle of a move is harmless: the new node is placed where the old one had got to and carries on, and a redraw of the same board changes nothing because the faces already belong where they are going. Input is never held up. What takes a click is a child of the face, so a face is clicked where it is drawn, and a new answer mid-move simply sets off the next move from there. Glows, Stack Marks and steppers are children of the face too and go with it; block lines, which are not, are worked out again each frame while anything moves.

**What does not move.** A rewind (undo, or a canceled cast) and a window of another size put every face at its place at once and leave no ghosts, because neither is something that happened in the game (ADR 0015). The snapshot says whether it came from a rewind, since an empty event list cannot tell a rewind from a quiet pass. Scrolling a line is not a move either: the screen tells Face Motion where the scroll carried each face. A permanent that leaves while scrolled out of sight gets no ghost, which would otherwise appear outside its line. The Hero's own clicks in a half-made combat selection do move things: an attacker eases into the lane when clicked.

**The clock.** Face Motion counts time a frame at a time and never counts a frame as more than a thirtieth of a second. The frame that rebuilds the board is a slow one, and timing a move from the wall clock let that frame eat the start of every move, which read as a jump (the first prototype did this). The curve is slow-fast-slow for the same reason: a curve that starts fast covers a quarter of a turn in its first frame.

**Pacing.** The Villain answers faster than a human can read, so the Encounter Presenter holds back collecting the Villain's next answer until the last snapshot has been up long enough (`ui/encounter/pacing.gd`). The wait comes from what that snapshot showed, never from the kind of answer that made it: nothing for no events or only bookkeeping (a step or turn starting, mana emptying, untapping, combat ending, effects ending, damage wearing off), 200 ms when the rest is only tapping and mana (270 at first; cut to 200 at the user's request, so a land now starts turning just before the last one finishes), and 500 ms for anything else. A pass that makes a spell resolve therefore waits as long as casting one. The mana wait is a little longer than a move, so each land finishes turning before the next starts. Because the answer is simply not collected yet, thinking time counts toward the wait, the screen never shows an old game, and the rule that nothing reads the engine while the worker has it is untouched. The wait applies only between two Villain answers: the Hero is asked the moment the Villain's answer is collected, and the Villain's first answer after the Hero's is not held back. Time spent paused counts toward the wait.

**A step is shown for a tenth of a second at least.** A turn nobody acts in used to go by in a frame, so the step buttons said nothing about where the game had been. A snapshot that shows a step starting is now left up for 100 ms (`Pacing.STEP_MSEC`) before the game moves on, whoever would move it. This is the one wait that is not only between two Villain answers: the Villain's answer is held back for it even straight after the Hero's, and a pass auto-pass makes for the Hero is put off for it. While that pass is put off the snapshot asks the Hero nothing, as while the Villain thinks, so options they are about to be passed over do not flash; their answers are refused, but undo, the stops and auto-pass still work, and changing a stop looks at the decision again without cutting the step short. A step where the Hero is really asked is not delayed: they are asked at once, and the tenth of a second is only owed before the game leaves the step. The presenter holds the step and not the screen, because a screen that walked its step marker along behind the game would show a step the game had already left. The untap and cleanup steps give nobody priority, so the engine goes through them inside one answer and no snapshot ever shows them; they are not held.

The presenter and Face Motion take their numbers as plain values that default to zero, and the screen sets the game's numbers only when it starts a real encounter (`use_game_pace`). Tests, the soak and `--screenshot` therefore never wait and never see a face part-way.

**Cards fly between zones.** A card that changes zones is drawn once, in the air, from where it was to where it lands, in place of fading out in one place and in at another. Card Flights (`ui/encounter/card_flights.gd`) is kept on the screen beside Face Motion and has the same shape: it holds no nodes, only a pose to start from and a pose to land at for each card, kept by the ID of the Game Object that lands, and the screen asks for each pose every frame and places a `FlightCard` there. The routes come from the snapshot's zone-change events, which link the object that left to the object the card became. A card that passed through a zone on the way has one route, and a card whose next move comes while it is still in the air carries on from where it has got to, so a spell that resolves before it reaches the Stack Window swerves to where it ends up.

Where a card was is read from what is still drawn, before the board is rebuilt; where it lands is read after, and again every frame, because the place can move under it: the hand makes room, a line is scrolled, a window is dragged. The card is put in the air before the rebuild so that what it lands on is drawn knowing it has not arrived. That face shows nothing until the card lands, and a pile shows the card that was under it. A permanent that flies away leaves no ghost; the ghost remains for a permanent that leaves with nowhere to fly to. A flight takes 0.35 seconds (`Pacing.FLIGHT_SECONDS`): longer than a move, since it goes further, and over before the Villain's next action. Cards a player draws together leave the library one at a time, 0.15 seconds apart (`Pacing.DRAW_GAP_SECONDS`), in the order drawn; the user asked for it after seeing them fly as one. Each waits on the library until its turn. A drawn card joins the hand when it lands, and not before: the hand is drawn without it while it is in the air, the card flies to the place it will take in the row as the row will be when it arrives, and when it lands the screen redraws and the cards already there ease apart from where they were, the one redraw that does not put the hand straight at its places. The Villain's hand gains its card back the same way. The user asked for it after seeing the hand open a gap for every card at once. A card that reaches a hand from anywhere else still lands on a face the hand already made room for.

A Flight Card is drawn as what the card was where it left and as what it is where it lands, one fading into the other with both solid half-way: a Card Face from the hand becomes a Battlefield Face, and a card back from a library turns face up for the Hero and stays a back for the Villain. The Stack Window shows an entry and no card, so a card put on the stack flies to the art at the left of its entry, shrinks to the art's size and fades out over the last quarter of the way, and a card leaving the stack fades in there. The first prototype faded it over the whole flight, which looked like a ghost crossing the board. Flight Cards are on a layer drawn over every window, by its `z_index` and not its place in the tree, because a click brings a window to the front of the tree. An entry of the Stack Window finds its place a frame after a redraw, and a card can leave the stack before that frame comes, so the screen remembers where each entry's art was last seen.

Nothing flies for a rewind or through the pregame, and a window of another size lands every card at once, as it puts every face at its place. A permanent that leaves while scrolled out of sight flies, small, from under the arrow at that end of its line. Like Face Motion, Card Flights is off unless the screen plays at the game's pace.

**Game Speed.** One setting scales every wait, move, flight and fade together: Slow (twice Pacing's numbers), Normal (one and a half times), Fast (Pacing's numbers as they are) and Blazing (half). Every number in this ADR is the one Fast plays at. The speeds were first Slow at one and a half times, Normal at Pacing's numbers, Fast at half and Instant at none; in play Normal felt hurried, so each name moved down one and the old Fast came back as Blazing in place of Instant. No speed stops the game from waiting any more: only a screen that is not paced does, which is how a test runs. A settings file that says Instant reads as Normal. It is chosen on the Pause Menu's settings page, applies at once to the encounter that is running, and is kept in `user://settings.cfg` (`GameSettings`). Only a screen that starts itself reads or saves that file; a screen made in a test uses none unless it is given a path, so the suite never depends on what the player chose. The file is read line by line and not as a `ConfigFile`, which prints an error for a file it cannot parse.

**Not yet.** Cards are not dealt or mulliganed through the air, and nothing is animated for a shuffle, damage or life. The hand keeps its own easing toward the cursor.

Two alternatives were rejected. Keeping the nodes and changing only what differs between snapshots would animate naturally, but it rewrites the screen and gives up the property that the board is a function of the snapshot alone. Queuing snapshots and playing each event as a scripted animation would let every event have its own flourish, but the screen would then show a game older than the one being decided, and every decision input would have to wait for the queue to drain.

# A click's meaning is worked out once, and the Glow is drawn from it

From 13c the Hero answers decisions by clicking things on the Encounter Screen. What a click on each object or player answers is worked out in one place, `DecisionClicks`, a pure class made from the Pending Decision and the Hero's Player View, with no Nodes in it. It gives each clickable Game Object a list of `ClickOption`s (an answer and its text), each player an answer, and each color of pool mana an answer. The Glow is drawn from that same object, so a thing that takes a click always glows, in the color of what the click does. A decision kind that still has no input of its own (A243) glows what it can choose but gives no click answers, and the Debug Decision Panel goes on answering it.

**Only what answers takes the mouse.** Faces are made ignoring the mouse. The screen gives a `ClickCatcher` to each face, and each player's panel, that has an answer; the catcher is a child of what it covers, brightens it under the cursor and shows the pointing hand. Everything else stays deaf, so a click on something that does nothing is silent rather than an error (A291). Right-click is left free for the inspector (13h).

**One answer is given at once; several open the Option Menu.** A permanent offering one thing does it on a single click with no confirmation, because undo and cancel already make a mis-click recoverable (A285). A permanent offering more opens the Option Menu where it was clicked, a row for each option, three rows showing and the rest scrolled to (A286). The menu is not only for abilities: kicker and the two halves of an adventure card will be rows of it too, which is why it is named for options (A307). While the cursor is over a row, the Info Panel shows that option in full in place of its prompt. A permanent with a mana ability and another ability glows green, and blue only when mana is all it offers (A306).

**The Info Panel says what is asked.** One bordered Region by the Hero's hand holds the prompt for the current decision, what is still owed while paying, the reason the last answer was refused, "Holding Priority" (shown as soon as the hold key is down, so the Hero knows the next spell will keep priority, A304), and the Cancel button for a cast in progress. Its text is worked out by `InfoPrompt`, also pure.

**Pausing is not collecting.** Escape opens the Pause Menu. A Villain thought already running cannot be stopped midway, so pausing lets it finish on its thread and simply does not collect it: no snapshot is handed out, the screen keeps drawing the one it has, and the presenter refuses the Hero's answers. Resuming collects the thought and carries on from exactly where the game was (A299). Conceding and quitting each ask once more, since neither can be undone, and quitting will carry the save with it when saving exists, so that a save can never be taken and then played on from (A297, A305).

Three alternatives were rejected. Letting each face work out its own click from the decision would have spread one rule over every kind of node and left the Glow free to disagree with it. Paying mana automatically was put off: it needs a policy for which lands to keep, and clicking each source had to feel right first (A287). Stopping the Villain's thread to pause was never an option the engine offers, and waiting for the thought before showing the menu would have made Escape feel slow against a Normal AI.

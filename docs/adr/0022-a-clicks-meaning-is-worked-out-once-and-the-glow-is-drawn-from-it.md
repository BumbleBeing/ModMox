# A click's meaning is worked out once, and the Glow is drawn from it

From 13c the Hero answers decisions by clicking things on the Encounter Screen. What a click on each object or player answers is worked out in one place, `DecisionClicks`, a pure class made from the Pending Decision and the Hero's Player View, with no Nodes in it. It gives each clickable Game Object a list of `ClickOption`s (an answer and its text), each player an answer, and each color of pool mana an answer. The Glow is drawn from that same object, so a thing that takes a click always glows, in the color of what the click does. Until 13e-2 a decision kind with no input of its own (A243) glowed what it could choose and was answered in the Debug Decision Panel; every kind has an input now, and the panel is gone (ADR 0030).

**Only what answers takes the mouse.** Faces are made ignoring the mouse. The screen gives a `ClickCatcher` to each face that has an answer. A player who can be chosen glows and is clicked on their portrait alone, never the whole panel, whose buttons have nothing to do with being a target; the catcher is a child of what it covers, brightens it under the cursor and shows the pointing hand. Everything else stays deaf, so a click on something that does nothing is silent rather than an error (A291). Right-click is left free for the inspector (13h).

**One answer is given at once; several open the Option Menu.** A permanent offering one thing does it on a single click with no confirmation, because undo and cancel already make a mis-click recoverable (A285). A permanent offering more opens the Option Menu where it was clicked, a row for each option, three rows showing and the rest scrolled to (A286). The menu is not only for abilities: kicker and the two halves of an adventure card will be rows of it too, which is why it is named for options (A307). While the cursor is over a row, the Info Panel shows that option in full in place of its prompt. A permanent with a mana ability and another ability first glowed purple alone, and blue only when mana was all it offered (A306); it now glows in both colors (below).

**The Info Panel says what is asked.** One bordered Region by the Hero's hand holds the prompt for the current decision, what is still owed while paying, the reason the last answer was refused, and the Cancel button for a cast in progress. "Holding Priority" used to be a small line there; it is now the Hold Panel, a box of its own opposite the Info Panel at the top left of the battlefield, in large type, shown exactly while the Hold Key is down (as soon as it is, so the Hero knows nothing will be passed for them, A304) and not for a hold that is still in force after the key is let go. Its text is worked out by `InfoPrompt`, also pure.

**Pausing is not collecting.** Escape opens the Pause Menu. A Villain thought already running cannot be stopped midway, so pausing lets it finish on its thread and simply does not collect it: no snapshot is handed out, the screen keeps drawing the one it has, and the presenter refuses the Hero's answers. Resuming collects the thought and carries on from exactly where the game was (A299). Conceding and quitting each ask once more, since neither can be undone, and quitting will carry the save with it when saving exists, so that a save can never be taken and then played on from (A297, A305).

**Combat is answered all at once, so its clicks build a selection.** Declaring attackers, declaring blockers and dividing combat damage each take one answer for the whole decision (ADR 0016), so a click there cannot be an answer. It changes a `CombatSelection`, a pure class that holds the attackers chosen, the blocks paired or the damage given so far, says what can be clicked next, and gives the answer when the Hero confirms with the pass button. The screen keeps it outside the nodes it redraws and makes a new one only when the decision itself changes, so a resize, a pause or a new snapshot of the same decision leaves it alone (A322). Undo takes back one click of it before it undoes an answer (A320). `DecisionClicks` still gives these decisions no click answers: what it says is what a click *answers*, and here no single click does.

**Combat is drawn where it happens.** A creature chosen to attack or block steps into the lane where it stands, upright, because only the engine taps it (A312). Once the engine has creatures attacking or blocking, they stand to the left of the rest of their row: attackers in object ID order, and blockers in the order of the attackers they block (ties in ID order), so no two block lines cross; while the Hero is still choosing, nothing changes places under the cursor. A line with an arrowhead runs from each blocker to its attacker, pale gold while it is being chosen and white once declared, and a line to a creature scrolled out of sight ends on the scroll arrow that points toward it (A327). Blockers were first drawn standing opposite their attackers (A315), but that rule could not hold together with the left-hand order, which the user preferred, so the lines carry the pairing alone. Damage is divided with a stepper on each recipient, starting from a split that kills as many as it can, with the attacked player's portrait as a recipient when the attacker has trample (A318, A321). The Villain's combat is drawn the same way.

**The stack is clicked and read in its window (13d-1).** A spell that can be targeted is an object like any other in `DecisionClicks`, so it glows and takes its click on its Stack Window entry: the entry's border turns the Glow's orange-red while its stripe keeps its own Stack Color, so it stays recognizable (A354). Each object's Stack Color is chosen when it arrives and kept in `StackColors` on the screen, outside the redrawn nodes, because a snapshot alone cannot say which hue an object was given (A335). The entry under the cursor draws Stack Marks on the board: a solid border on an ability's source and a dashed one on each target, since solid reads as settled and dashed as about to happen. A mark hides the Glow without removing it, so a marked target still takes its click and glows again when the cursor leaves (A346). The Info Panel shows the hovered entry in full, as it does a row of the Option Menu, until the inspector takes that over (A342, 13h).

**Ordering triggers and choosing a replacement effect have their own inputs (13d-1).** Simultaneous triggers are one answer for the whole decision, so they are built in a `TriggerOrder`, kept on the screen as a `CombatSelection` is: the triggers stand above the stack with dashed borders, the top one resolving first to match the window, and the pass button confirms (A339). They were first moved with ▲ and ▼ buttons; since 13e-2 each is dragged to its place (below). Triggers that all read the same and have the same cause are ordered by the presenter without asking, as it passes priority for the Hero, because their order changes nothing (A344). They are compared by their generated words, since each card holds its own copy of an ability. Choosing which replacement effect applies first is a single answer, so one click gives it: a Zone Window titled for the event lists each effect with its source and what it does, and cannot be closed while the choice is asked. It is not answered by clicking sources on the battlefield, because a shield made by a resolved spell has its source in a graveyard and two shields can share one source (A340).

**A card in hand is dragged out or double-clicked, never clicked once (13e-1).** A card that can be cast or played glows like anything else a decision offers, but `DecisionClicks` keeps what it offers apart (`hand_options_of`), so it gets no `ClickCatcher` and one click does nothing: a card that rises under the cursor is too easy to click by accident (A371). A double-click plays it. A press that moves three pixels picks it up as the Drag Card, which the screen keeps outside the redrawn nodes like the `CombatSelection`, so the card stays held while the board is rebuilt under it. Any card can be picked up at any time, because rearranging the hand has nothing to do with the game; only a glowing card can be played. Let go anywhere above the hand's strip, a card is played if it can be and otherwise goes back without a word, by the same rule that makes a click on something that answers nothing silent. Let go over the strip, the Info Panel included, it is put down where the other cards made room. Moving over the left panels, a right-click and the Pause Menu opening all put it back. A card with several ways to be played waits where it was let go while the Option Menu asks which, and goes back if the menu closes without an answer (A372); no card has two ways yet, so that path is tested with a hand-built decision.

A press on a card is taken in the screen's `_input()`, ahead of everything beneath the card: a card that has risen under the cursor lies over the Hero's lands, and a land that can be tapped would otherwise take a press on the card's top edge, which is where a card is grabbed. The second build got this wrong and was harder to pick cards up in than the prototype. Only what is drawn over the hand comes first, an open window or the Option Menu, so a card is never picked up through one. Once a card is pressed, the screen follows the mouse over windows and buttons too, until the button comes up. A press on the empty board is taken in the screen's `_gui_input()`, which only gets what nothing else took.

Two more things made presses miss, and both showed only under a real mouse. The hand eases toward the cursor over several frames, so a press made on arriving, on the strip a card was about to rise into, was tested against where the card was still drawn and found nothing; a press now counts on the nearest card where it is drawn or where the cursor is sending it, and the easing takes its weight from the frame's length so a slow frame does not leave the hand behind. And a second try at a drag soon after a first arrives marked as a double-click, which did nothing on a card that could not be played; such a press is now a press like any other. No press is ever lost to a slow frame: the events queue in order with their own positions, and a press, a move and a release that arrive together are handled as they happened.

**Nothing is rebuilt while a card is held.** Rebuilding the board took about 40 ms then, more than two frames (ADR 0021 has how that came down to about 7). The first prototype rebuilt it whenever the gap in the hand moved, and the second still did once on pickup; both read as a stutter under the cursor. So picking a card up lifts its own face to a layer above the windows, the rest of the hand is only told where to go and eases there, and putting the card down slides the same face back into the row. A play does rebuild, because the game changed, but by then nothing is held.

**The hand's order is the Hero's (13e-1).** The engine's hand has an order no rule reads, so the screen keeps its own, the Hand Order, by Card Identity: a card that leaves the hand and comes back is a new Game Object, and only its Card Identity says it is the same card. A new card joins at the right end. A card that left keeps its place for a rewind to give back, so a canceled cast or an undo puts it where it was; one that comes back any other way joins at the right end like a drawn card. Undo does not undo a rearrangement.

**Triggers are dragged into order (13e-2).** The ▲ and ▼ buttons went: two ways to do one thing is two things to test, and the arrows were two of the characters the game's font lacks, which cost a millisecond each time one was laid out (A393). An entry has a drawn grip and is dragged from anywhere on it. It feels as the hand does (A394): a press becomes a drag after three pixels, the entry follows the cursor up and down only and never leaves the list, the others make room, letting go puts it down, and a right-click puts it back. One drag is one step for undo however far it went, so `TriggerOrder` moves a trigger from one place to another rather than swapping neighbors. The entries live in a `TriggerList`, a plain Control that places them by hand: a container would snap them to their places, and here they ease. Nothing is rebuilt while one is held or when one is put down, or for an undo; the list is told the new order and the same entries ease to it. While an entry is pressed the screen follows the mouse in `_input()`, off the window too, exactly as it does for a card of the hand. The list does not scroll by itself when it is longer than its window.

**Cards to discard are clicked in the hand's strip (13e-2).** At cleanup the board has to stay in sight, so the choice is made where the cards are. One click on a card of the hand still answers nothing by itself (A371): it changes a `CardPicks`, kept on the screen like the `CombatSelection`, and the pass button confirms once enough are chosen (A400). A click is a press let go where it was made, counted on release, so a press that moves three pixels is still a drag that rearranges the hand; a double-click is two clicks. A chosen card stands a quarter of its height above the row with a white Glow, and a click on it lets it go (A401). With enough chosen the rest stop glowing and a click on one does nothing. Nothing is rebuilt for a click, so the card eases up; because it is drawn higher, it is also hit-tested higher, by the rule that a press counts where a thing is drawn. `DecisionClicks` still glows the cards that can be discarded and still gives them no click answers.

**A Glow eases in and out.** A Glow or Stack Mark fades in, fades out, and crossfades when its color changes, over `Pacing.EASE_SECONDS` (0.15 s), easing so it never pops. The board is rebuilt on every redraw, so the screen remembers what each object glowed before and draws a Glow that has not changed at once; only a change eases, or every Glow on the board would flash with each snapshot. A Glow taken away is gone at once to anything that asks and is only drawn while it fades. Tests and screenshots give it no time, as they give Face Motion none. Windows ease the same way, on the same curve and time, through one `Easing` class: the Stack Window, the replacement choice window, the Emblems windows, the Option Menu and the Pause Menu; and the outline that says which player has priority eases from one panel to the other. A window closing is closed at once, to the code that asks and to the mouse, and is only drawn while it fades; one opened again partway comes back from where it was.

**Something that offers two kinds of thing glows in both colors.** One color could only say one of the things a click or a drop would offer, and A306's rule hid a land's mana behind its other ability. So `DecisionClicks` keeps every kind an object has, in a fixed order (playing it, an ability to activate, mana), and its Glow is split between the colors of the first two: the first along the top and left, the second along the bottom and right, with a short blend at the two corners where they meet. A fixed order means the same pair always looks the same: purple over blue for a permanent with a mana ability and another, green over purple or green over blue for a card in hand. Three kinds at once show the first two, because three colors do not read at the size of a card. Only playing, abilities and mana can share an object; every other decision gives each thing one kind. A split was chosen by eye over a gradient, which turned gray-green between green and purple, and over two rings, where the outer color drowned the inner one against a card's black border. The split is drawn by `SplitOutline`, and a Glow of one color is drawn as it always was. What takes one color from a Glow, the arrow of a line scrolled out of sight, takes the first.

**An ability used from the hand is offered when the card is let go.** It is one of the card's hand options, after the ways to play it, so a drop or a double-click on a card that can be cast and has such an ability opens the Option Menu with both, and a card with only the ability uses it at once. A held card therefore glows in every color it has, each made lighter where letting go would act (A375), since letting go no longer only plays it. No card has an ability used from the hand yet, so this is tested with hand-built decisions, as the two ways to play a card are (A372).

Three alternatives were rejected. Letting each face work out its own click from the decision would have spread one rule over every kind of node and left the Glow free to disagree with it. Paying mana automatically was put off: it needs a policy for which lands to keep, and clicking each source had to feel right first (A287). Stopping the Villain's thread to pause was never an option the engine offers, and waiting for the thought before showing the menu would have made Escape feel slow against a Normal AI.
